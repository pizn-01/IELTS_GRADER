# IELTS Task 2 Essay Types: Structures That Score

Most Task Response problems start before a single sentence is written: the candidate misreads the question type and answers a different task than the one asked. **IELTS AI Tutor by IELTSGRADER** built this hub to fix that first, most fixable mistake — spot the type, apply the right paragraph map, and only then worry about vocabulary.

There are five recurring IELTS Writing Task 2 essay types. Each rewards a different paragraph job, even though four paragraphs is still the safest default length for nearly all of them.

## Why identifying the type comes first

Task Response is graded on whether you answered *this* question, not whether you wrote fluent English about the topic. A well-written essay that discusses both sides when the prompt demanded a single opinion, or that lists advantages and disadvantages when the prompt asked for causes and solutions, is off-task no matter how advanced the grammar is. Ninety seconds spent naming the type and assigning paragraph jobs is the highest-value planning step in the whole 40 minutes. For the general planning method behind this hub, see [best essay structure for IELTS Task 2](/blog/best-essay-structure-ielts-task-2).

## The 5 IELTS Task 2 essay types at a glance

| Type | Typical prompt signal | Default paragraphs |
|------|-----------------------|---------------------|
| Agree / disagree (extent) | "To what extent do you agree or disagree?" | 4 |
| Discuss both views | "Discuss both these views and give your own opinion." | 4 (sometimes 5) |
| Advantages / disadvantages | "What are the advantages and disadvantages…?" or "Do the advantages outweigh…?" | 4 |
| Problem / solution | "What problems does this cause and what solutions…?" | 4 |
| Two-part question | Two separate question marks in one prompt | 4 (sometimes 5) |

Full template pages for each type are linked in the sections below, and every one links back here so you can compare types side by side.

Occasionally a prompt seems to blend two types — for example, an "outweigh" question that also asks "what can be done about the disadvantages" leans toward problem/solution in its second half. When that happens, treat the *final* instruction sentence as the deciding one, since that is usually the exact sentence an examiner checks your answer against.

## 1. Agree / disagree (extent) essays

**Recognition cues:** "To what extent do you agree or disagree", "Do you agree or disagree", a single stated opinion with no second view offered.

**Paragraph jobs:** introduction states your extent (fully / largely / partly agree); body 1 gives your strongest reason; body 2 gives a second reason or a concession that still supports your side; conclusion restates the extent.

**Common TR trap:** starting with "I agree" and drifting into "but it also depends" by the conclusion — the extent must stay consistent from the first sentence to the last.

Full walkthrough with a mini sample: [To what extent agree essay: Band 8 template](/blog/to-what-extent-agree-template). Complete annotated model: [Band 7.5 opinion essay](/blog/task-2-opinion-essay-band-7-5).

## 2. Discuss both views essays

**Recognition cues:** "Discuss both these views and give your own opinion", two named perspectives in the prompt itself.

**Paragraph jobs:** introduction paraphrases both views and previews your opinion; body 1 develops view A fairly; body 2 develops view B and states (or confirms) your opinion; conclusion closes with a clear stance.

**Common TR trap:** "fence-sitting" — describing both views accurately but never actually giving an opinion, which the task explicitly requires.

Full walkthrough: [Discuss both views essay structure Band 7.5+](/blog/discuss-both-views-structure).

## 3. Advantages and disadvantages essays

**Recognition cues:** "What are the advantages and disadvantages…?" or "Do the advantages outweigh the disadvantages?"

**Paragraph jobs:** introduction paraphrases the topic and, if the prompt asks "outweigh", states your weighing decision immediately; body 1 covers advantages; body 2 covers disadvantages (or the heavier side, if outweigh-style); conclusion confirms which side wins or gives a balanced summary if the prompt only asked for both sides.

**Common TR trap:** treating a pure "advantages and disadvantages" prompt as though it demands a personal opinion, or treating an "outweigh" prompt as though a balanced list is enough — the two sub-types need different conclusions.

Full walkthrough: [Advantage disadvantage essay: 4-paragraph formula](/blog/advantage-disadvantage-4-paragraph).

## 4. Problem and solution essays

**Recognition cues:** "What problems does this cause?", "What solutions can you suggest?", "What are the causes of this problem and what measures could be taken to solve it?"

**Paragraph jobs:** introduction paraphrases the issue and previews that you will cover causes/problems and solutions; body 1 explains one or two problems or causes; body 2 offers solutions that match those specific problems; conclusion briefly links problem to solution.

**Common TR trap:** solutions that do not connect to the problems named in body 1, or solutions so vague ("the government should do something") that they add no real content.

Full walkthrough: [Problem solution essay structure for IELTS Task 2](/blog/problem-solution-essay-structure).

## 5. Two-part question essays

**Recognition cues:** two distinct questions inside one prompt, each ending in its own question mark, often with no shared word like "advantages" or "views" tying them together.

**Paragraph jobs:** introduction paraphrases both questions; body 1 answers question 1 fully; body 2 answers question 2 fully; conclusion offers a short synthesis if useful.

**Common TR trap:** answering only one question thoroughly and giving the second a single throwaway sentence — Task Response treats a missing part as a major gap, regardless of how strong the first answer is.

Full walkthrough: [Two-part question essay structure IELTS Task 2](/blog/two-part-question-essay-structure).

## The 4-paragraph default map

Across all five types, this shape covers most prompts inside a 40-minute limit:

1. **Introduction (2–3 sentences):** paraphrase the topic, then answer the specific demand of that type (extent, both views + opinion, outweigh decision, problem/solution preview, or both parts of a two-part question).
2. **Body 1:** first reason, first view, advantages/problems side, or answer to question 1 — claim, explanation, one concrete example.
3. **Body 2:** second reason, second view + opinion, disadvantages/solutions side, or answer to question 2 — same claim/explain/example pattern.
4. **Conclusion (1–2 sentences):** restate the position or synthesis. No new arguments.

Body paragraph mechanics apply the same way regardless of type: [best essay structure for IELTS Task 2](/blog/best-essay-structure-ielts-task-2) covers the claim → explain → example formula in depth, and [Band 6 vs Band 7 Task 2](/blog/band-6-vs-7-task-2) shows how development quality — not paragraph count — separates the two bands.

## When five paragraphs work better

Five paragraphs earn their place only when a third body genuinely has its own job:

- **Discuss both views:** body 1 = view A, body 2 = view B, body 3 = your opinion developed on its own, if the prompt rewards a fuller personal stance than one shared sentence can hold.
- **Two-part questions:** occasionally a short third paragraph is useful if question 2 has two distinct sub-strands that do not fit cleanly beside question 1's answer.

Five paragraphs that are each thin and underdeveloped score worse than four well-developed ones. Paragraph count is not the lever — paragraph *job clarity* is.

## Decision flow: name your type in under a minute

Work through this in order and stop at the first match:

| Step | Question to ask | If yes |
|------|-----------------|--------|
| 1 | Does the prompt contain two separate question marks with unrelated questions? | Two-part question |
| 2 | Does it say "advantages and disadvantages" or "outweigh"? | Advantages / disadvantages |
| 3 | Does it mention "problems" and "solutions" (or "causes" and "measures")? | Problem / solution |
| 4 | Does it say "discuss both views" or name two opinions plus "give your own opinion"? | Discuss both views |
| 5 | Does it say "to what extent do you agree/disagree" or simply "do you agree"? | Agree / disagree |

If a prompt seems to match two rows, the specific wording in the final sentence usually decides it — that sentence is the actual instruction the examiner checks against.

Practising this decision table on past-paper prompts, without writing full essays, is one of the highest-value five-minute drills available: read ten prompts, name the type for each in under 20 seconds, then check yourself against the recognition cues above. Candidates who can do this reliably rarely lose Task Response marks to a misread question on exam day.

## Try this yourself

**Prompt bank — one per type:**

1. *Agree/disagree:* "Some people think university education should be free for everyone. To what extent do you agree or disagree?"
2. *Discuss both views:* "Some people prefer to spend their lives doing the same things. Others prefer new activities and challenges. Discuss both views and give your opinion."
3. *Advantages/disadvantages:* "More people are choosing to work from home permanently. Do the advantages of this trend outweigh the disadvantages?"
4. *Problem/solution:* "Many cities are experiencing severe air pollution. What are the causes of this problem, and what measures could be taken to solve it?"
5. *Two-part:* "Some people leave their home country to study or work abroad. Why do people make this decision? Is this a positive or negative trend?"

Pick two, name the type out loud before you plan, then write a full 4-paragraph plan for each in under 5 minutes. Check your finished essay with the [IELTS Task 2 checker](/ielts-task-2-checker) and look at the Task Response comment first — it should confirm you matched the type correctly.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many IELTS Task 2 essay types are there?

Five recurring types appear across past papers: agree/disagree, discuss both views, advantages/disadvantages, problem/solution, and two-part questions. Some guides split hybrids further, but these five cover the paragraph-job logic you need.

### Should every essay have four paragraphs?

Four is the safest default for all five types. Five paragraphs can help discuss-both-views or two-part prompts when a third body has a genuinely separate job, but extra paragraphs without development do not raise Task Response.

### How do I quickly identify the essay type under exam pressure?

Read only the final instruction sentence first — it usually names the task directly ("to what extent", "discuss both views", "advantages and disadvantages", "solutions", or two question marks). Use the decision table above as a checklist during practice until it becomes automatic.

### What is the biggest Task Response mistake across all five types?

Answering a different task than the one set — for example, giving only an opinion when the prompt asked for both views, or offering solutions that do not match the problems you described.

### Can AI tell me which essay type I am looking at?

Yes. Paste a prompt into the [Task 2 checker](/ielts-task-2-checker) alongside your essay and the Task Response feedback should reflect whether your structure matched the specific type the question demanded.

### Where should I start if I only have one week to practise?

Start with the type you find hardest to plan quickly, work through its dedicated template above, write two timed essays of that type, then check both with AI before moving to the next type.
