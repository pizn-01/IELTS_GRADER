# GT Formal Letter Checklist: What Examiners Want

General Training Task 1 is a letter, and formal letters are where many candidates lose easy marks. They write a friendly email voice, miss a bullet point, or forget a clear purpose. This checklist from **IELTS AI Tutor by IELTSGRADER** is designed for complaint, request, application, and formal enquiry letters.

Use it before you submit practice letters to the [essay checker](/ielts-essay-checker), and compare with a high-band model such as the [Band 8 complaint letter](/blog/gt-letter-sample-band-8-complaint).

## First: confirm it is a formal letter

Formal tone is usually required when you write to:

- A company, council, or institution
- Someone you do not know by name (or know only as a role: *Dear Sir or Madam*, *Dear Hiring Manager*)
- Situations involving complaints, applications, official requests, or problems with a service

If the prompt says you are writing to a friend, switch to informal style instead. Mixing tones is a Task Achievement problem.

## The master checklist (print this)

### A. Purpose and opening

- [ ] First sentences state **why** you are writing
- [ ] Tone matches formal expectations (no slang, no text-speak)
- [ ] Correct greeting (*Dear Sir or Madam* / *Dear Mr Smith* / *Dear Ms Lee*)
- [ ] If name unknown, you plan to end with *Yours faithfully*
- [ ] If name known, you plan to end with *Yours sincerely*

### B. Bullet coverage (Task Achievement critical)

- [ ] Bullet 1 fully addressed with enough detail
- [ ] Bullet 2 fully addressed with enough detail
- [ ] Bullet 3 fully addressed with enough detail
- [ ] No bullet answered with a single vague sentence if the prompt expects explanation
- [ ] Extra information does not replace a missing bullet

### C. Organisation

- [ ] Short opening paragraph for purpose
- [ ] Separate paragraphs for different bullets (or clear separation)
- [ ] Logical order (often: situation → details → request/action)
- [ ] Clear closing paragraph stating what you want next

### D. Formal language

- [ ] Full forms preferred in most places (*I am*, not *I’m*, contractions are often too casual)
- [ ] Polite modality where appropriate (*I would be grateful if…*, *Could you please…*)
- [ ] Precise vocabulary for the situation (complaint, refund, appointment, documentation)
- [ ] No chatty fillers (*anyway*, *by the way*, emoji-style emphasis)

### E. Closing

- [ ] Polite closing line (*I look forward to your response.*)
- [ ] Correct sign-off
- [ ] Name on the next line
- [ ] Word count at least 150

### F. Grammar and accuracy

- [ ] Consistent tense for the timeline of events
- [ ] Clear references (*this issue*, *the above appointment*)
- [ ] No meaning-blocking errors in key request sentences

## Bullet points: the #1 GT Task 1 failure

Examiners score Task Achievement on whether you cover the required information. A beautifully written letter that ignores one bullet is still incomplete.

**Method that works under time pressure:**

1. Underline the three bullets in the prompt.
2. Number them 1–3 in your plan.
3. Tick each after you write it.
4. Spend your last two minutes checking ticks, not adding fancy vocabulary.

## Tone bank: formal phrases that sound natural

### Opening purpose

- I am writing to complain about…
- I am writing to enquire about…
- I am writing to request…
- I am writing with regard to…

### Explaining a problem

- On [date], I purchased / attended / booked…
- Unfortunately, …  
- Despite contacting your staff, the issue remains unresolved.
- This has caused considerable inconvenience because…

### Making a request

- I would be grateful if you could…
- I am requesting a full refund / replacement / written explanation.
- Please confirm in writing by…
- I would appreciate your urgent attention to this matter.

### Closing

- I look forward to hearing from you at your earliest convenience.
- Please do not hesitate to contact me on [details] should you require further information.

Use two or three of these, not all of them stacked in one paragraph.

## What “enough detail” looks like

**Too thin (risk Band 6 TA):**  
> The room was bad. Please fix it.

**Stronger:**  
> The room I was given on 3 May was advertised as a quiet double with a sea view; however, it faced a construction site and the air conditioning did not work throughout my two-night stay. I would like a partial refund and an explanation of why I was not moved after I reported the problem at reception.

Detail makes the bullet complete and supports Lexical Resource with precise situation language.

## Formal vs semi-formal: quick boundary

| Feature | Formal | Semi-formal (e.g. neighbour / colleague you know) |
|---------|--------|--------------------------------------------------|
| Greeting | Dear Sir or Madam / Dear Ms X | Dear John / Dear Mr X (context-dependent) |
| Contractions forms | Avoid | Sometimes acceptable |
| Directness | Polite and measured | Can be a little warmer |
| Sign-off | Yours faithfully / sincerely | Kind regards / Best regards often fine |

If unsure, check the recipient relationship in the prompt first.

## 20-minute timing checklist

| Time | Task |
|------|------|
| 0–2 min | Identify tone + underline bullets |
| 2–4 min | Plan one point per bullet + desired outcome |
| 4–17 min | Write letter |
| 17–20 min | Tick bullets, check greeting/sign-off, count words |

## Common formal-letter mistakes

1. Starting with background and never stating the purpose clearly  
2. Sounding angry and rude in complaints (firm is fine; insults hurt tone)  
3. Using *Dear friend* or first names for unknown recipients  
4. Ending with *Yours sincerely* after *Dear Sir or Madam* (conventionally mismatched)  
5. Writing an essay-style conclusion instead of a clear requested action  
6. Falling under 150 words because bullets were answered in one line each

## Mini self-mark after every practice letter

Score yourself quickly:

- **TA:** Did I cover every bullet with enough detail? (yes/no)  
- **CC:** Can a reader find purpose, details, and request easily?  
- **LR:** Did I use situation-precise words?  
- **GRA:** Are request sentences accurate?

Any “no” on TA means rewrite before polishing vocabulary.
