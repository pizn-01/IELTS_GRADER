# From Mock Exam to 14-Day Sprint: The IELTS AI Tutor Journey

A mock writing test without a plan is just a stressful hour. The high-value journey is **mock → report → 14-day sprint → retest**. This article from **IELTS AI Tutor by IELTSGRADER** maps that product journey so you know what to do on Day 0, Day 1, and Day 14.

## Why this journey works

Mocks reveal performance under time pressure. Reports reveal the bottleneck criterion. Sprints create deliberate practice. Retests prove whether the bottleneck moved.

Skip any stage and you get common failure modes:

- Mock only → anxiety without improvement  
- Report only → insight without action  
- Random drills only → activity without measurement  

## Stage 1: Mock exam (Day 0)

### Setup

- Academic or GT materials matching your registration  
- 60 minutes, no pauses  
- Task 1 ~20 minutes, Task 2 ~40 minutes  

Use the [IELTS mock writing test](/ielts-mock-writing-test) or self-time and submit tasks afterward.

### Rules

- No dictionary mid-mock  
- No rewriting after time for the “official” mock version  
- Match paper vs computer format when possible  

More timing detail: [mock writing practice guide](/blog/ielts-mock-writing-practice-guide).

### Output of Stage 1

Two completed tasks ready for evaluation, not a feeling, a script.

## Stage 2: Tutor report (Day 0 evening or Day 1)

Open the report in this order:

1. Lowest criterion on Task 2 (usually your Writing driver)  
2. Lowest criterion on Task 1  
3. Fix cards / concrete issues  
4. Overall bands (context only)  

Learn the reading method in [feedback to study plan](/blog/feedback-to-study-plan). Criteria primer: [how writing is scored](/blog/how-ielts-writing-is-scored).

### Choose ONE sprint theme

Examples:

- Coherence topic sentences  
- Task Response development  
- Task 1 overviews  
- GT bullet completeness  
- Article/agreement grammar family  

If everything is “a bit weak,” still pick one. Plateau help: [stuck at 6.5](/blog/stuck-at-band-6-5-plateau).

## Stage 3: 14-day sprint (Days 1–14)

### Design principles

- Same theme for 14 days  
- Mix drills + timed writing + one rewrite early  
- Mid-check around Day 7  
- Full retest on Day 14  

### Universal 14-day skeleton

| Days | Focus |
|------|--------|
| 1–2 | Drill the theme (no full mock) |
| 3 | Rewrite the weaker mock task |
| 4–5 | More drills + one timed single task |
| 6 | Light review / error log |
| 7 | Mid-check (timed Task 2 or Task 1) |
| 8–10 | Continue theme; add light second skill only if Day 7 improved |
| 11 | Second rewrite or new timed task |
| 12–13 | Consolidation; sleep and short reviews |
| 14 | Full mock retest |

### Theme packs (pick one)

**If CC is the theme:** follow [14-day Coherence plan](/blog/coherence-cohesion-14-day-plan) as your drill engine.

**If TR is the theme:** study [Band 6 vs 7](/blog/band-6-vs-7-task-2); enforce explain+example daily.

**If LR is the theme:** use [lexical Band 6→7](/blog/lexical-resource-band-6-to-7) or [trends vocabulary](/blog/task-1-trends-vocabulary).

**If TA (letters) is the theme:** [formal letter checklist](/blog/gt-formal-letter-checklist) every practice letter.

**If TA (charts) is the theme:** overview-first drills + [bar chart sample](/blog/task-1-bar-chart-band-7).

## Stage 4: Retest and compare (Day 14)

Run another full mock. Compare:

| Metric | Mock 1 | Mock 2 |
|--------|--------|--------|
| Target criterion | | |
| Other criteria | | |
| Timing comfortable? | | |
| Same mistake family? | | |

### How to interpret results

- **Target criterion up, others stable** → sprint worked; choose next theme.  
- **Everything flat** → drills were too passive (reading ≠ writing); increase rewrite volume.  
- **Target up, timing worse** → keep theme but add timed micro-tasks.  
- **Scores noisy** → look at 2–3 task trends, not one overall number ([dual grading trust](/blog/dual-ai-grading-explained)).  

## Where learning editions fit

After several mocks and sprints, repeated mistake patterns can power [personalized learning editions](/blog/personalized-learning-editions-guide), curated study paths from your error families ([error taxonomy](/blog/ielts-writing-error-taxonomy)).

The journey stays the same: measure → focus → practise → remeasure.

## Sample calendar (Coherence sprint)

- **Day 0:** Full mock  
- **Day 1:** Topic sentence clinic (6 sentences)  
- **Day 2:** One-idea paragraph splits  
- **Day 3:** Rewrite mock Task 2  
- **Day 4–5:** Referencing + anti-linker-chain drills  
- **Day 7:** Timed Task 2 check  
- **Day 8–11:** Maintain CC; one GT/Academic Task 1 day so Task 1 does not decay  
- **Day 14:** Full mock  

## Common journey mistakes

1. Changing the sprint theme on Day 3 because a new report mentioned vocabulary  
2. Skipping the rewrite of the original mock task  
3. Doing two full mocks a day (fatigue, no learning)  
4. Ignoring Task 1 entirely during a Task 2 sprint  
5. No Day 14 retest, so you never close the loop  

## Energy and recovery (yes, this counts)

Writing sprints fail when sleep collapses. Keep Days 12–13 lighter on purpose. A tired Day 14 mock produces noisy data and false conclusions about your sprint. If you are unwell on Day 14, delay the retest 48 hours rather than collecting a garbage baseline.

## Handwriting and capture notes

Paper-based candidates should keep OCR clean when uploading sprint work, [handwritten OCR tips](/blog/handwritten-essay-ocr-tips). Otherwise Day 7 “grammar disasters” may be camera problems, not English problems.


## Try this yourself

**Prompt:** After your next mock, write a 14-day calendar with only one criterion focus.

2. [Check your essay free](/ielts-mock-writing-test) (or the Task 1/2 checker if relevant).
3. Rewrite only the lowest criterion once, then re-check.

## Frequently asked questions

### What should I do after a Writing mock?

Identify the lowest criterion and start a short focused sprint.

### Why 14 days?

Long enough to change one habit; short enough to retest before drifting.

### Can I train all criteria at once?

Usually slower. Fix the cap first, then the next.
