# Personalized Learning Editions: Study Paths from Your Mistakes

Generic IELTS advice fails for a simple reason: your bottleneck is not everyone else’s. **Learning editions**, study paths built from your repeated mistake patterns, are how a tutor product personalises practice without pretending every student needs the same 50 tips.

This guide from **IELTS AI Tutor by IELTSGRADER** explains what learning editions are, how mistake patterns feed them, and how to use them alongside mocks and checkers.

## What a learning edition is

A learning edition is a focused study path organised around a **pattern family**, such as:

- Coherence: weak topic sentences and mixed paragraphs  
- Task Response: undeveloped examples  
- Academic Task 1: missing overviews  
- GT letters: incomplete bullets / tone slips  
- Grammar: articles and agreement recurring  

It is not a random content dump. It is a curated sequence: explain → drill → apply → recheck.

## Why personalization needs patterns (not one essay)

One essay can be a bad day. Three essays with the same Coherence flags are a diagnosis.

That is why editions work best after:

1. A baseline check or [mock writing test](/ielts-mock-writing-test)  
2. At least one rewrite cycle  
3. A second timed task confirming the same family  

See the pattern catalogue in [IELTS writing error taxonomy](/blog/ielts-writing-error-taxonomy).

## From report → pattern → edition

| Stage | What you see | What it becomes |
|-------|----------------|-----------------|
| Single report | Fix cards | Immediate rewrite tasks |
| Multiple reports | Repeated flags | Pattern label |
| Edition | Structured lessons/drills | 7–14 day path |
| Retest | New mock/check | Proof of transfer |

This sits inside the wider [mock → sprint journey](/blog/mock-exam-to-14-day-sprint) and [feedback to study plan](/blog/feedback-to-study-plan) method.

## Example edition sketches

### Edition: Coherence Builder

**For:** CC ≤ other criteria  
**Includes:** topic sentence clinic, paragraph job maps, referencing drills, anti-mechanical-linker practice  
**Anchor guide:** [14-day Coherence plan](/blog/coherence-cohesion-14-day-plan)  
**Exit check:** timed Task 2 with CC notes improving

### Edition: Task Response Depth

**For:** clear opinions but thin bodies  
**Includes:** claim–explain–example templates, prompt analysis, extent-language practice  
**Anchor guides:** [Band 6 vs 7](/blog/band-6-vs-7-task-2), [Band 7.5 opinion sample](/blog/task-2-opinion-essay-band-7-5)  
**Exit check:** body paragraphs with concrete mechanisms

### Edition: Academic Task 1 Clarity

**For:** missing overviews / poor comparisons  
**Includes:** overview-only drills, trend paraphrase, selection practice  
**Anchor guides:** [trends vocabulary](/blog/task-1-trends-vocabulary), [bar chart Band 7](/blog/task-1-bar-chart-band-7)  
**Exit check:** TA notes cite a clear overview

### Edition: GT Letter Control

**For:** missed bullets or tone mismatches  
**Includes:** bullet-tick routine, formal phrase bank, complaint/request structures  
**Anchor guides:** [formal letter checklist](/blog/gt-formal-letter-checklist), [Band 8 complaint](/blog/gt-letter-sample-band-8-complaint)  
**Exit check:** all bullets covered with detail

### Edition: Lexical Precision

**For:** repetition and awkward “advanced” words  
**Includes:** collocation banks, paraphrase without thesaurus spam  
**Anchor guide:** [lexical resource Band 6 to 7](/blog/lexical-resource-band-6-to-7)  
**Exit check:** less repetition; fewer wrong collocations

## What learning editions are not

- Not a promise of a specific official band  
- Not a replacement for timed practice  
- Not an excuse to ignore Task 1 while only studying Task 2 tips  
- Not infinite content with no exit exam  

They are tutoring structure. Accuracy expectations: [is AI tutoring accurate?](/blog/is-ai-ielts-tutoring-accurate).

## How to follow an edition without losing exam realism

Keep at least one timed task each week even during drill-heavy editions. Skills that only work untimed often collapse in the exam.

Suggested weekly mix inside an edition:

- 3 drill sessions  
- 1 rewrite  
- 1 timed task  
- Optional: mid-week Task 1/letter maintenance day  

## Measuring whether an edition worked

Use criterion movement, not vibes:

| Signal | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| Target criterion up on 2 checks | Transfer happening |
| Fix cards change topic | Old pattern fading |
| Same fix cards return | Drills too passive, write more |
| Overall up, target flat | Lucky variance, stay on theme |

Borderline overall scores need calm interpretation, [dual grading explained](/blog/dual-ai-grading-explained), [6.5 plateau](/blog/stuck-at-band-6-5-plateau).

## Building a manual edition if you are just starting

Even before a fully productised path, you can DIY:

1. List your last three reports’ top fix themes.  
2. Circle the theme that appears twice+.  
3. Assign 14 days using the skeletons above.  
4. Retest.  

That is already personalization.

## Edition sequencing (do not stack three at once)

Good order examples:

- CC edition → TR depth edition (structure first, then richer ideas)  
- TA overview edition → LR trends edition (Task 1 clarity, then nicer language)  
- GRA agreement edition → complex sentence range (control first)  

Risky order:

- LR rare-word hunting while paragraphs are still mixed  
- Advanced grammar drills while Task Response ignores half the question  

When in doubt, fix the lowest criterion edition first. Plateau logic: [stuck at 6.5](/blog/stuck-at-band-6-5-plateau).

## Time budgets inside an edition

A realistic edition week for working adults:

| Block | Minutes |
|-------|---------|
| Drill session | 25–35 |
| Timed task | 20–40 |
| Rewrite | 25–40 |
| Report review + log | 10–15 |

Four short sessions beat one heroic Sunday. Pair with plan intensity guidance in [practice plans explained](/blog/ielts-writing-practice-plans-explained).
