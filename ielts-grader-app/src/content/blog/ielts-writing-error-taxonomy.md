# IELTS Writing Error Taxonomy: Mistakes Your AI Tutor Flags

Not all Writing mistakes are equal. A missing overview, a wrong collocation, and an article error hurt different criteria, and need different fixes. This taxonomy from **IELTS AI Tutor by IELTSGRADER** groups common issues the way a tutor reads your script: by criterion first, then by pattern.

Use it to interpret reports from the [essay checker](/ielts-essay-checker) or [Task 2 checker](/ielts-task-2-checker).

## How to use this taxonomy

1. Find your lowest criterion on a recent report. 
2. Scan only that section below. 
3. Pick the 1–2 patterns that match your writing. 
4. Drill those for a week using [feedback to study plan](/blog/feedback-to-study-plan). 

Trying to fix the entire list at once is how candidates stay busy without improving.

---

## 1. Task Response / Task Achievement errors

### TR1: Partial answer

**What it looks like:** Discusses only one side of a two-part question; ignores “advantages and disadvantages”; gives no opinion when required.

**Fix:** Underline every task instruction word before planning.

### TR2: Undeveloped ideas

**What it looks like:** Topic sentence + one short claim + move on. Common at Band 6, see [education sample](/blog/task-2-sample-band-6-education).

**Fix:** Force explain + example in every body paragraph.

### TR3: Position drift

**What it looks like:** Introduction agrees; conclusion becomes neutral or opposite.

**Fix:** Write your extent stance in one line and paste it above the page while writing.

### TR4: Generic examples

**What it looks like:** “For example, in many countries…” with no mechanism.

**Fix:** Name a concrete scenario (school policy, workplace rule, household habit).

### TA5: Missing Task 1 overview (Academic)

**What it looks like:** Data listed; no summary of main trends.

**Fix:** Two overview sentences before details. Model: [bar chart Band 7](/blog/task-1-bar-chart-band-7).

### TA6: Missed letter bullet (GT)

**What it looks like:** Beautiful tone; one prompt bullet ignored.

**Fix:** Tick bullets. Checklist: [GT formal letter checklist](/blog/gt-formal-letter-checklist).

### TA7: Wrong letter tone

**What it looks like:** Slang in a complaint to a manager; stiff formality to a friend.

**Fix:** Identify recipient relationship first.

## 2. Coherence & Cohesion errors

### CC1: Mixed-paragraph syndrome

**What it looks like:** One paragraph contains two unrelated main ideas.

**Fix:** One job per paragraph. Plan with labels.

### CC2: Mechanical linker chains

**What it looks like:** Firstly / Moreover / Furthermore / In addition with no logical progress.

**Fix:** Ban empty linkers for a week; use because / however / as a result. Plan: [14-day CC plan](/blog/coherence-cohesion-14-day-plan).

### CC3: Weak or missing topic sentences

**What it looks like:** Paragraph starts with a detail or example.

**Fix:** First sentence must predict the paragraph.

### CC4: Poor referencing

**What it looks like:** Same noun repeated eight times; or unclear *this* with no antecedent.

**Fix:** Practise *this approach / these measures / such a policy*.

### CC5: Conclusion introduces new arguments

**What it looks like:** Brand-new example appears only at the end.

**Fix:** Conclusions summarise and close, no new case studies.

### CC6: Illogical order

**What it looks like:** Example before the claim; contrast before the idea being contrasted.

**Fix:** General → specific → significance.

---

## 3. Lexical Resource errors

### LR1: Repetition loops

**What it looks like:** *increase / people / government / environment* recycled endlessly.

**Fix:** Small paraphrase banks. Guides: [trends vocabulary](/blog/task-1-trends-vocabulary), [LR Band 6→7](/blog/lexical-resource-band-6-to-7).

### LR2: Thesaurus stuffing

**What it looks like:** Rare words used inaccurately (*ameliorate the students*).

**Fix:** Prefer precise common academic collocations.

### LR3: Wrong collocation

**What it looks like:** *make a meeting*, *strong rain*, *big damage*.

**Fix:** Learn verb–noun pairs by topic, not isolated words.

### LR4: Informal lexis in Academic essays

**What it looks like:** *kids*, *stuff*, *a lot of guys think*.

**Fix:** *children*, *issues*, *many people argue*.

### LR5: Degree mismatch in Task 1

**What it looks like:** *skyrocketed* for a 2% rise.

**Fix:** Match adverb/adjective strength to the data.

### LR6: Copying the rubric

**What it looks like:** Introduction repeats the question almost word for word.

**Fix:** Paraphrase prompt key terms in the first two sentences.

---

## 4. Grammatical Range & Accuracy errors

### GRA1: Agreement errors

**What it looks like:** *People is*, *The number of students are*.

**Fix:** Error log; rewrite only agreement mistakes for three days.

### GRA2: Article problems

**What it looks like:** Missing *the* with unique references; extra articles with plurals.

**Fix:** Weekly article clinic on your own sentences.

### GRA3: Tense timeline breaks

**What it looks like:** Task 1 jumps randomly between past and present; narrative essays mix past forms without reason.

**Fix:** Decide timeline before writing; circle verbs in review.

### GRA4: Complex sentence collapse

**What it looks like:** Long sentences with *although… but*, broken relative clauses.

**Fix:** Two clean complex patterns you can control; then expand.

### GRA5: Punctuation that blocks meaning

**What it looks like:** Run-ons; comma splices in key opinion sentences.

**Fix:** Full stop discipline in conclusions and topic sentences.

### GRA6: Range without control

**What it looks like:** Forced passives and conditionals that confuse the reader.

**Fix:** Accuracy first; add range only where meaning stays clear. See [Band 6 vs 7](/blog/band-6-vs-7-task-2).

## Cross-criterion “combo” mistakes

Some issues hit more than one band descriptor:

| Pattern | Criteria affected |
|---------|-------------------|
| Memorised template off-topic | TR + CC + LR |
| Listing every Task 1 number | TA + CC + LR |
| Angry informal complaint letter | TA + LR |
| Endless introduction, thin bodies | TR + CC |

Your AI tutor may flag the symptom in one criterion; your study plan should still address the root.

## Error taxonomy → weekly plan

| If most flags are… | Spend the week on… |
|--------------------|---------------------|
| TR/TA | Question analysis + development drills |
| CC | Paragraph jobs + topic sentences |
| LR | Collocation banks + paraphrase |
| GRA | Personal error log + controlled complex sentences |

After patterns repeat across essays, [learning editions](/blog/personalized-learning-editions-guide) can group them into a focused study path.

## Worked example: one essay, four families

Imagine a Task 2 script with these tutor flags:

- Body 2 is only three sentences and restates the claim (TR2)
- Three paragraphs start with *Moreover* (CC2)
- *Important* appears nine times (LR1)
- *People is* appears twice (GRA1)

A weak response is “study everything.” A tutor response is:

1. This week: TR2 only, expand every body with explanation + example 
2. Next week: CC2, ban empty linkers; rebuild topic sentences 
3. Keep a mini log for GRA1 so it does not return 
4. Build a 15-word paraphrase bank later for LR1 

That sequencing is how taxonomy becomes a plan instead of a panic list. Full method: [feedback to study plan](/blog/feedback-to-study-plan) and [mock to 14-day sprint](/blog/mock-exam-to-14-day-sprint).

## Severity: meaning-blocking vs cosmetic

Not every flag deserves equal time.

**Meaning-blocking (fix first):**

- Missing letter bullet / missing overview 
- Position that contradicts itself 
- Grammar errors that confuse who did what 
- Paragraphs so mixed the reader cannot follow 

**Cosmetic (fix after the above):**

- One repeated linker 
- A slightly informal word in an otherwise clear academic sentence 
- A rare article slip that does not hide meaning 

Band 7 scripts can still contain small cosmetic issues. Band 6 scripts often mix blocking problems with cosmetic ones, and students polish cosmetics first because they are easier to notice.

## Task 1 vs Task 2: do not mix taxonomies blindly

A “thin example” note on Task 2 is a TR issue. On Academic Task 1, inventing an example about *why* sales rose is usually a TA problem (speculation). On GT letters, “thin” often means a bullet answered without detail, still TA.

Keep module context clear with [Academic vs GT writing](/blog/academic-vs-general-training-writing) and [TR vs TA decoded](/blog/task-response-vs-achievement).

## Building a personal error codebook

After three checked tasks, create a one-page codebook:

| Code | My example sentence | Corrected version | Criterion |
|------|---------------------|-------------------|-----------|
| GRA1 | People is worried… | People are worried… | GRA |
| CC1 | (paste mixed paragraph) | (split version) | CC |
| LR3 | make a research | conduct research | LR |

Review the codebook for five minutes before every timed essay. This is more effective than rereading generic grammar books. Collocation support: [lexical resource Band 6 to 7](/blog/lexical-resource-band-6-to-7).
