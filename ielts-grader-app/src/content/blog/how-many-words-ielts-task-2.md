# How Many Words Should You Write in IELTS Task 2?

You should write **at least 250 words** for IELTS Writing Task 2. Aim for **260–290 words**: enough depth for Band 7 Task Response without rushing Grammar and Coherence in 40 minutes. Under 250 risks a Task Response penalty; far past 320 often means unfinished conclusions or uncontrolled errors.

This guide from **IELTS AI Tutor by IELTSGRADER** explains how examiners treat word count, what happens if you write too little or too much, and how to hit the range under timed conditions.

## How many words for IELTS Task 2? (quick table)

| Word count | Examiner risk | Tutor advice |
|------------|---------------|--------------|
| Under 250 | Task Response may be capped; ideas look incomplete | Never stop here on purpose |
| 250–260 | Minimum met; development may still be thin | Add one example sentence per body paragraph |
| 260–290 | Best balance of depth and control | Target this range in mocks |
| 300–320 | Fine if quality holds | Only if you finish on time |
| 350+ | Time pressure; more GRA/LR errors | Cut planning fluff, not development |

Task 1 is different: Academic reports and GT letters need **at least 150 words**. Do not steal Task 2 time to pad Task 1 past ~180 unless Task 1 is unfinished.

## What examiners actually penalise

Word count is not a separate criterion. It shows up inside **Task Response**.

- **Under 250:** You often under-develop ideas or skip a part of the question. Examiners can treat the response as incomplete.
- **Barely 250 with empty sentences:** “In conclusion, this is important for society” repeated does not buy Task Response marks.
- **Very long essays:** No automatic bonus. If the extra words add new, relevant support, fine. If they add repetition, off-topic stories, or grammar collapse, Lexical Resource and Grammar suffer.

Examiner-style view: a 240-word essay with clear position and two developed ideas can still look stronger than a 380-word essay that never finishes the second body paragraph cleanly. Length without development does not raise Task Response.

## Before / after: thin 220 words vs controlled 275

**Prompt:** Some people believe university education should be free for everyone. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

### Under-length pattern (≈220 words — Task Response risk)

> I agree that university should be free. Poor students cannot pay fees. If education is free, more people can study and get better jobs. Also, society needs skilled workers. In conclusion, free university is good for equality.

Clear opinion, almost no development. This is why “how many words” questions matter: short scripts usually fail the *depth* part of Task Response, not just a number on a counter.

### Controlled Band 7-style length (≈275 words — one body only shown)

> I partly agree that tuition should be free for students from low-income households, but not as an unlimited benefit for every applicant. When fees rise, first-generation students often leave higher education not because of ability, but because families cannot absorb the cost. For example, a student who must work full-time evenings to pay fees has less time for reading and coursework, which widens the achievement gap. That said, “free for everyone” still requires public funding, so governments usually need means-tested support and clear eligibility rather than an open door. A more realistic policy is generous grants for need, plus affordable loans for others, so access improves without removing all personal investment.

Same prompt, developed cause → example → nuance. That is what the extra 40–50 words should buy you.

## How to hit 260–290 under the clock

1. **Plan 4–5 minutes:** position + two body ideas + one example each.
2. **Write body paragraphs first** if you panic on introductions.
3. **Count once at ~30 minutes**, not every sentence.
4. **If under 250 at minute 35:** add one concrete example, not three new ideas.
5. **If over 320 with 5 minutes left:** write a short conclusion and stop editing vocabulary.

## Does handwriting vs computer change word count?

On paper tests, examiners estimate length; they do not need a perfect count, but clearly short answers are obvious. On computer-delivered IELTS, a word counter may appear — still aim for quality in the 260–290 zone, not a race to 400.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many words should you write in IELTS Task 2?

At least 250. Most candidates should aim for 260–290 so ideas are developed without running out of time or flooding the essay with errors.

### Is there a penalty for writing more than 300 words?

There is no automatic penalty for length alone. The risk is time pressure and more Grammar or Coherence mistakes. Quality beats bulk.

### How many words for Task 1?

At least 150 words for Academic Task 1 and GT letters. About 160–180 is usually enough if the overview and key features are clear.

### Do examiners count every word?

They check that you meet the minimum. On computer tests you may see a counter; on paper they estimate. Do not waste exam minutes counting by hand.

### What if I finish at 248 words?

Add one short example or clarifying sentence. Do not submit knowingly under 250 if you still have two minutes.
