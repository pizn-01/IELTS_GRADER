# Difference Between Band 6 and Band 7 IELTS Writing

The jump from Band 6 to Band 7 in IELTS Task 2 is one of the most common goals — and one of the most misunderstood. Many candidates add longer words, longer essays, and more “Moreover” sentences, then stay stuck at 6 or 6.5.

**The real difference:** Band 7 writing develops ideas fully, organises paragraphs with clear logic, uses precise collocations, and controls complex grammar. Band 6 usually has a clear position but under-developed ideas, mechanical linking, repetitive vocabulary, and frequent small errors.

**IELTS AI Tutor by IELTSGRADER** looks at the same four criteria examiners use. This guide shows side-by-side patterns you can copy into your next practice essay — then [check your essay with AI](/ielts-essay-checker) to see which gap is yours.

## Quick snapshot: Band 6 vs Band 7

| Area | Typical Band 6 | Typical Band 7 |
|------|----------------|----------------|
| Task Response | Position clear; ideas under-developed | Position clear; ideas extended with support |
| Coherence | Basic structure; linking sometimes mechanical | Logical flow; paragraphs focused |
| Lexical Resource | Adequate but repetitive / occasional wrong word | More precise collocations; less repetition |
| Grammar | Mix of simple/complex; frequent small errors | Better control of complex forms; fewer meaning-blocking errors |

Your overall Task 2 band is the average of these four. Raising the weakest criterion is usually the fastest path from 6 to 7. Full formula: [how IELTS writing scores are calculated](/blog/how-ielts-writing-is-scored).

## Task Response: stating vs developing

### Band 6 pattern

> Free university education is good because poor students can study. If university is expensive, only rich people go. This is not fair.

The opinion is clear, but the paragraph stops at the claim. There is no concrete example, no explanation of *how* fairness is affected, and no acknowledgement of limits.

### Band 7 pattern

> Making tuition free can widen access for students from low-income families, who might otherwise abandon higher education to take low-paid work. For example, when fees rise sharply, enrolment among first-generation students often falls first, not because ability drops, but because families cannot absorb the cost. That said, “free” still requires public funding, so governments usually need clear eligibility rules rather than an unlimited open door.

Same topic, clearer development: cause → example → nuance. That is Task Response at Band 7.

**Tutor tip:** After every topic sentence, ask “How?” and “So what?” If you cannot answer in two more sentences, the idea is still Band 6 depth.

## Coherence & Cohesion: connectors vs logic

### Band 6 pattern

> Firstly, technology helps students. Moreover, it is useful in class. Furthermore, teachers can use it. In addition, it saves time. Therefore, technology is important.

The linkers are present, but the paragraph does not progress. Each sentence restates “technology is useful.”

### Band 7 pattern

> Technology can improve classroom feedback when teachers use it to track common errors across a cohort. Instead of waiting for end-of-term exams, students receive short practice tasks matched to weak grammar points. As a result, revision becomes targeted rather than generic, provided the tools are used for diagnosis, not only for entertainment.

Fewer mechanical transitions; clearer cause-and-effect. Coherence improves because ideas *build*, not because more synonyms of “also” appear.

## Lexical Resource: rare words vs right words

### Band 6 pattern

> The government should ameliorate the education system to facilitate students to gain knowledge in a better way.

“Ameliorate” and “facilitate” look advanced but the sentence is stiff and imprecise.

### Band 7 pattern

> Governments can improve school quality by funding teacher training and reducing oversized classes, so students get more individual feedback.

Everyday academic English, accurate collocations (*funding teacher training*, *individual feedback*). Band 7 Lexical Resource is often quieter than candidates expect.

## Grammatical Range & Accuracy: attempting vs controlling

### Band 6 pattern

> Although online learning have many benefits, but students is feeling isolated and they don’t can concentrate easily.

Attempts at complex structure, but agreement, double conjunction (*Although… but*), and modal errors reduce clarity.

### Band 7 pattern

> Although online learning offers flexibility, many students still feel isolated, which can make sustained concentration harder without a clear study routine.

Complex sentence with controlled grammar. Occasional minor slips can still appear at Band 7; systematic errors that confuse meaning usually keep GRA at 6.

## Side-by-side mini essays (same prompt)

**Prompt:** Some people think the best way to increase road safety is to increase the minimum legal age for driving. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

### Band 6 extract (body paragraph)

> I agree that increasing age can make roads safer. Young people drive too fast and they have accidents. If the age is higher, they will be more mature. Also, older drivers have more experience. So this policy is good for society and it can reduce problems.

Clear position, limited development, repetitive lexis (*age*, *good*), simple chaining.

### Band 7 extract (body paragraph)

> Raising the minimum driving age may reduce accidents among the youngest drivers, who statistically take more risks and have less experience judging speed and distance. In countries that delayed licensing, casualty rates in the newly restricted age group often fell. However, age alone is a blunt tool: a poorly trained 21-year-old can still be dangerous, while a well-trained 18-year-old may drive carefully. Therefore, higher age limits work best alongside mandatory practice hours and stricter testing, not as a single solution.

Position + evidence-style support + concession + precise conclusion for the paragraph.

## What usually keeps people at 6.5

Many candidates sit between the two bands:

- Ideas are better than Band 6, but one paragraph stays thin
- Vocabulary improves, but Task Response still misses part of the question
- Grammar is mostly clear, but Coherence suffers from weak topic sentences

If that sounds familiar, read [why am I stuck at Band 6.5](/blog/stuck-at-band-6-5-plateau) and use criterion-level reports instead of chasing overall score only.

## A two-week practice loop to move toward Band 7

**Week 1: Diagnose and deepen Task Response**

- Write two timed Task 2 essays (40 minutes, 250+ words)
- For each body paragraph, force: claim → explanation → example → mini-conclusion
- Rewrite the weaker essay once after feedback

**Week 2: Control Coherence and Lexical Resource**

- Plan paragraphs with one topic sentence each before writing
- Replace three repeated nouns/verbs with precise alternatives (not thesaurus spam)
- Keep a personal error log for articles, agreement, and tense

Track whether your *lowest* criterion rises. That movement matters more than a lucky overall spike.

## Mistakes that look like Band 7 but score Band 6

1. **Memorised introductions** that do not match the question
2. **Balanced essays** when the prompt asks for a clear opinion and you never commit
3. **Example dumping** (stories with no link back to the argument)
4. **Over-complex sentences** that collapse into errors
5. **Ignoring “discuss both views and give your opinion”** structure

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the difference between Band 6 and Band 7 IELTS writing?

Band 7 develops ideas with explanation and examples, organises paragraphs logically, uses precise vocabulary, and controls complex grammar. Band 6 often has a clear position but under-developed ideas, mechanical linking, and more frequent errors.

### Is Band 6.5 closer to 6 or to 7?

It depends on your criterion profile. Many 6.5 scripts are “half way” — two criteria near 7 and two stuck at 6. Closing the lowest criterion is what moves you to overall 7.

### Can I jump from Band 6 to Band 7 in two weeks?

Possible if the gap is mainly Task Response development or Coherence (fixable with focused rewrites). Less likely if Grammar or Lexical Resource errors are systematic across every sentence — those usually need a longer error-log habit.

### Should I use more advanced vocabulary to get Band 7?

Usually no. Precise, natural collocations beat rare words used incorrectly. Overreaching vocabulary is a common reason candidates stay at Band 6 Lexical Resource.
