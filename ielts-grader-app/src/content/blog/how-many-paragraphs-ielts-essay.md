# How Many Paragraphs Should an IELTS Essay Have?

For IELTS Writing Task 2, **four paragraphs** is the most reliable structure: introduction, two body paragraphs, and a short conclusion. That layout fits the 40-minute limit and keeps Coherence & Cohesion clean. Five paragraphs can work for discuss-both-views essays; two or three long blocks usually hurt clarity and Task Response.

This guide from **IELTS AI Tutor by IELTSGRADER** shows what examiners reward in paragraphing, when to use four vs five blocks, and how weak paragraph jobs keep candidates at Band 6.

## How many paragraphs in IELTS Task 2? (summary)

| Structure | Typical use | Risk |
|-----------|-------------|------|
| 4 paragraphs (intro + 2 body + conclusion) | Opinion, advantages/disadvantages, problem/solution | Lowest risk for most prompts |
| 5 paragraphs (intro + 3 body + conclusion) | Discuss both views + opinion; two-part questions | Fine if each body has one clear job |
| 3 paragraphs | One giant body | Coherence suffers; ideas blur |
| 6+ short paragraphs | Bullet-style writing | Looks fragmented; weak development |

Task 1 is different: Academic reports often use **overview + 2 data paragraphs** (sometimes 4 blocks total with intro). GT letters use greeting + purpose + 2–3 body paragraphs + closing.

## Why paragraph count matters for Coherence

Examiners score **Coherence & Cohesion**, not “number of paragraphs” as a separate mark. But paragraphing is how they see organisation.

Band 6 pattern: paragraphs exist, but each one mixes two jobs (opinion + counter-argument + example + new topic).

Band 7 pattern: each paragraph has **one main idea**, a clear topic sentence, and sentences that build on that idea.

See the linker-vs-logic contrast in [Band 6 vs Band 7 Task 2](/blog/band-6-vs-7-task-2) and the [14-day Coherence plan](/blog/coherence-cohesion-14-day-plan).

## The standard 4-paragraph map

1. **Introduction (2–3 sentences):** paraphrase the question + clear position (or outline for discuss-both-views).
2. **Body 1:** strongest reason / first view — claim → explain → example.
3. **Body 2:** second reason / opposing view + your stance — same development pattern.
4. **Conclusion (1–2 sentences):** restate position; no new arguments.

For “discuss both views and give your opinion,” many high-scoring scripts still use four paragraphs by putting view A in body 1 and view B + opinion in body 2 — or five paragraphs if opinion needs its own short body.

## Before / after: one messy block vs clear paragraph jobs

**Prompt:** Some people think children should start learning a foreign language in primary school. Others think it should wait until secondary school. Discuss both views and give your opinion.

### Band 6 paragraphing (everything in one body)

> Primary school is better because children learn fast and their brains are flexible and also secondary school students are busier with exams moreover parents want early education but some teachers say young children get confused and I think early is better because of the future job market and travel.

Multiple ideas, no paragraph boundaries, weak referencing. Coherence stays mid-band even if vocabulary is fine.

### Band 7 paragraphing (two focused bodies)

**Body 1:** Supporters of early language learning argue that younger children acquire pronunciation and basic structures more naturally when lessons stay light and playful. For example, short songs and games can build listening confidence before grammar becomes the focus. Starting later can mean students treat the language as just another exam subject.

**Body 2:** However, delaying until secondary school can work if primary years prioritise literacy in the first language. Crowding the timetable with a foreign language may reduce time for reading and maths, which still underpin later academic success. On balance, I support an early start only when schools keep the load light and protect core literacy.

One job per paragraph. That is the real answer behind “how many paragraphs.”

## Common paragraphing mistakes

- **Memorised four-paragraph templates** that ignore the question type
- **Conclusion that repeats the intro word-for-word**
- **Body paragraphs without topic sentences**
- **One example essay style:** story with no link back to the argument
- **Five one-sentence paragraphs** that never develop

## Frequently asked questions

### How many paragraphs should an IELTS essay have?

Four is the safest default for Task 2: introduction, two bodies, conclusion. Use five when the question clearly needs three separate body jobs.

### Can I write only three paragraphs?

You can, but one long body often mixes ideas and lowers Coherence. Prefer splitting into two focused body paragraphs.

### How many paragraphs for Task 1?

Academic Task 1 commonly uses an introduction, an overview, and two detail paragraphs. GT letters vary by bullet points, usually 4–5 short sections including greeting and closing.

### Do I lose marks for five paragraphs?

No. Marks fall when paragraphs lack a clear purpose or fail to develop ideas — not because you used five instead of four.

### Should every paragraph be the same length?

No. Bodies should be the longest. Intro and conclusion stay short. Uneven length is normal if development is balanced across bodies.
