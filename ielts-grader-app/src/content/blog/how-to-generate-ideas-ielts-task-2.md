# How to Generate Ideas for IELTS Writing Task 2?

To generate ideas for IELTS Writing Task 2, spend **3–5 minutes planning** with a fixed frame: clarify the question type, take a position, then invent **two reasons each with one concrete example**. You do not need brilliant originality — examiners reward relevant, developed ideas under Task Response, not rare opinions.

This guide from **IELTS AI Tutor by IELTSGRADER** gives a repeatable ideation method, shows Band 6 “empty ideas” vs Band 7 development, and a practice drill you can run today.

## How to generate ideas for IELTS Task 2 (framework)

| Step | Time | Output |
|------|------|--------|
| 1. Decode the question | 30–45 sec | Opinion? Both views? Causes/solutions? |
| 2. Decide your position | 30 sec | Agree / partly / disagree / outweigh |
| 3. List two body ideas | 1–2 min | One sentence each |
| 4. Add one example each | 1 min | School, city, workplace, or research-style generalisation |
| 5. Check coverage | 30 sec | Did you answer every part? |

If step 3 is blank, use prompt stems: **Who is affected? What happens next? What is the cost? What is the alternative?**

## Why “no ideas” is usually a Task Response problem

Candidates often have ideas but reject them as “too simple.” Band 7 Task Response prefers a simple claim that is **explained and exemplified** over a clever claim that stays abstract.

Empty Band 6 idea:

> Education is important for society and the economy.

Developed Band 7 idea:

> Wider access to vocational training can reduce youth unemployment because employers can hire candidates who already have job-ready skills, which lowers hiring risk for small businesses.

Same topic family; second version has mechanism + stakeholder. Compare more pairs in [Band 6 vs Band 7](/blog/band-6-vs-7-task-2).

## Before / after: panic list vs usable plan

**Prompt:** Some people think governments should spend more on public parks and sports facilities. Others say money should go to hospitals and schools. Discuss both views and give your opinion.

### Panic notes (unusable)

> parks good / health / kids / hospitals important / education / taxes / I don’t know

### 4-minute plan (usable)

- **View A:** Parks/sports → public health prevention; example: free outdoor gyms in dense cities
- **View B:** Hospitals/schools → urgent services and literacy; example: understaffed clinics delay treatment
- **Opinion:** Prioritise health/education funding first; parks as secondary when basic services meet minimum standards
- **Paragraph map:** Body1 = parks view; Body2 = hospitals/schools + my priority

That plan already contains Task Response structure. Paragraph jobs: [how many paragraphs](/blog/how-many-paragraphs-ielts-essay).

## Idea banks by theme (start here, then personalise)

Do not memorise full essays. Memorise **angle types**:

- **Education:** access, quality, cost, skills vs theory
- **Technology:** productivity, privacy, inequality of access
- **Environment:** cost of prevention vs cleanup, individual vs policy
- **Health:** prevention, lifestyle, public vs private provision
- **Work:** flexibility, automation, training

For each angle, keep one “for” and one “against” example ready. Topic samples for models: [Band 7 technology](/blog/task-2-sample-band-7-technology), [Band 6.5 environment](/blog/task-2-sample-band-6-5-environment).

## Examiner-style checks before you write

Ask:

1. Does each idea answer **this** question (not a neighbouring one)?
2. Can I extend it with *because* + *for example*?
3. Am I repeating the same idea twice with new adjectives?
4. For two-part questions, do I have material for **both** parts?

If idea 2 is a twin of idea 1, replace it — repetition caps Task Response and Coherence.

## Try this yourself

**Prompt:** In many countries, people are living longer. What problems does this cause, and what solutions can you suggest?

Set a **4-minute timer**. Produce only a plan (no full essay): two problems + one example each; two solutions + one example each. Then write the essay in 36 minutes targeting 260–290 words ([word count guide](/blog/how-many-words-ielts-task-2)).

## Frequently asked questions

### How to generate ideas for IELTS Writing Task 2?

Decode the question, lock a position, invent two reasons, add one example each, and verify you covered every part — all in about four minutes before writing.

### What if my mind goes blank in the exam?

Use stems: who is harmed, what happens next, what it costs, what the alternative is. Write the simpler idea with a strong example rather than hunting for a “smart” opinion.

### Do my examples need to be real statistics?

No. Clear, realistic illustrations are enough. Fake precise percentages you cannot defend can hurt trust; general but logical examples are safer.

### Should I memorise ideas for common topics?

Memorise angles and example types, not full paragraphs. Memorised intros that miss the question lower Task Response.

### How many ideas do I need?

Two well-developed body ideas beat four undeveloped ones. Depth over breadth within 40 minutes.
