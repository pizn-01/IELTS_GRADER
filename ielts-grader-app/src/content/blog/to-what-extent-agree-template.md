# To What Extent Agree Essay: Band 8 Template

"To what extent do you agree or disagree?" is not the same instruction as "Do you agree or disagree?" — it asks *how far* you agree, and Task Response rewards a stated, sustained extent more than a flat yes/no. This template from **IELTS AI Tutor by IELTSGRADER** covers extent language, a concession paragraph that strengthens rather than weakens your position, and a mini Band 8-style sample you can copy the architecture from.

## The unique practice prompt for this template

**Prompt:** Many companies now allow employees to work from home permanently instead of coming into an office. To what extent do you agree that this is a positive change for the modern workplace?

This template builds one full plan and a mini sample around this remote-work prompt so you can see extent language applied to a specific, current topic rather than a generic placeholder.

## Extent language: fully, largely, partly

Your opening sentence should commit to one point on this scale and stay there until the conclusion:

| Extent | Sample phrase | What body 2 usually does |
|--------|----------------|---------------------------|
| Fully agree | "I completely agree that…" | Second independent reason, no concession needed |
| Largely agree | "I largely agree, although…" | Reason plus one limiting condition |
| Partly agree / partly disagree | "I agree to some extent, but…" | Genuine weighing of both sides, ending on your lean |
| Largely disagree | "I mostly disagree, though…" | Reason plus acknowledgement of a narrow exception |
| Fully disagree | "I strongly disagree that…" | Second independent reason against the statement |

Picking "largely agree" or "partly agree" is not weaker than "fully agree" — examiners reward a *specific, consistent* extent far more than an extreme one. What sinks Task Response is starting at "agree" and quietly finishing at "it depends" with no stated boundary.

## The 4-paragraph extent essay map

| Paragraph | Job |
|-----------|-----|
| Introduction | Paraphrase the topic; state your extent in one clear sentence |
| Body 1 | Your strongest supporting reason, developed with an example |
| Body 2 | Second reason, **or** a concession paragraph that still supports your extent |
| Conclusion | Restate the same extent — no softening, no new claims |

This is the same core map used across every Task 2 type; see the [full 4-paragraph default](/blog/task-2-essay-types-hub#the-4-paragraph-default-map) and the [Band 7.5 opinion sample](/blog/task-2-opinion-essay-band-7-5) for a complete annotated model.

## The concession paragraph: disagree a little, still win

A concession paragraph acknowledges a real limitation of your position, then closes by reaffirming your extent. It is one of the fastest ways to lift Task Response above a flat, one-sided Band 6 answer, because it shows you can weigh evidence rather than just assert an opinion.

**Structure of a concession paragraph:**

1. Name the strongest opposing point honestly (do not build a weak "straw man").
2. Explain briefly why it has some truth.
3. Return to your extent with a reason the concession does not defeat.

### Weak concession (Band 6 pattern)

> Some people say office work is better for teamwork, and that is true, but working from home is still good.

This restates without engaging — "but it is still good" is not an argument.

### Stronger concession (Band 7+ pattern)

> It is true that spontaneous, in-person collaboration can be harder to replicate over video calls, and some creative teams do lose momentum without a shared room. However, this is largely a management and tooling problem rather than an inherent flaw of remote work: teams that schedule structured collaboration sessions report similar output to office-based peers. For this reason, I still consider permanent remote work a largely positive shift for most knowledge-work roles.

Notice the concession does not abandon "largely agree" — it narrows the objection, then reasserts the extent with a reason.

## Mini sample: introduction and body 1

**Introduction:**

> Permanent remote work has moved from an emergency measure to a standard employment option at many companies. I largely agree that this shift benefits the modern workplace, provided organisations invest in deliberate communication rather than assuming remote teams will function identically to office ones.

**Body 1:**

> The clearest advantage of permanent remote work is the recovered time and flexibility for employees who previously commuted daily. A worker who saves ninety minutes a day can redirect that time toward family responsibilities, rest, or further study, all of which support long-term wellbeing and retention. Companies that offer this flexibility also report wider access to talent, since location no longer restricts hiring to commuting distance from a single office. That combination of individual benefit and organisational advantage is why remote work functions as more than a short-term convenience.

Body 2 would add either a second independent reason or the concession paragraph shown above, followed by a conclusion that restates "largely agree."

## Common extent-essay traps

- **Silent drift:** starting "I agree" and ending "it depends" without ever naming a boundary.
- **Balanced-essay confusion:** treating an extent prompt like a discuss-both-views prompt and giving 50/50 weight to each side with no lean — this under-answers the specific instruction. Compare with the true both-views task: [discuss both views essay structure](/blog/discuss-both-views-structure).
- **Extreme without support:** claiming "fully agree" but only restating the claim rather than developing a reason.
- **Ignoring the concession opportunity:** a flat two-reason essay can still score well, but a well-built concession paragraph often reads as more mature reasoning.

## Quick self-check before you submit

Before finishing a timed extent essay, check these four points in order:

1. **Underline your extent word** in the introduction (fully / largely / partly / mostly / strongly) and circle the same word or its clear synonym in the conclusion — they must match.
2. **Count your developed reasons.** Two fully developed reasons (or one reason plus one concession) beat three reasons that each get only a single sentence.
3. **Check your concession, if you used one**, actually returns to your stated extent rather than trailing off into a neutral summary.
4. **Read only your topic sentences in order.** If they do not obviously build toward the same conclusion, the essay may be drifting even if each paragraph looks fine in isolation.

This four-point check takes under two minutes and catches most of the drift and development gaps that keep extent essays capped at Band 6.

## Try this yourself

**Prompt:** Some people believe that examinations are the fairest way to assess a student's ability. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

1. Choose your extent in one sentence before writing anything else.
2. Plan body 1 (independent reason) and body 2 (second reason or concession) separately — label which one you chose.
3. Write the full essay in 40 minutes, then [score your draft against Task Response](/ielts-task-2-checker) and confirm the comment reflects a consistent extent from introduction to conclusion.

## Frequently asked questions

### What does "to what extent do you agree" actually ask for?

It asks how far you agree — fully, largely, partly, or not at all — not simply whether you agree. Your introduction should state that specific extent and your conclusion should match it exactly.

### Is "partly agree" a weaker answer than "fully agree"?

No. Examiners reward a consistent, well-supported extent at any point on the scale. A well-argued "partly agree" typically outscores a "fully agree" that only restates the claim without development.

### How is this different from a discuss-both-views essay?

Discuss-both-views asks you to develop both perspectives fairly before giving an opinion. An extent essay centres on your own position throughout, even when a concession paragraph acknowledges the other side briefly. See [discuss both views essay structure](/blog/discuss-both-views-structure) for the contrast.

### Do I need a concession paragraph in every extent essay?

No — two independent reasons for your extent are enough to score well. A concession paragraph is one strong option, not a requirement, and works best when you can name a real limitation rather than a weak, invented one.

### Can I change my extent between the introduction and conclusion?

No. Task Response penalises position drift. Decide your extent before writing and use the conclusion only to restate it, not to soften or reverse it.
