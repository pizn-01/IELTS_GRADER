If you only look at your overall Writing band, you are flying without instruments. Examiners, and a good tutor, score IELTS Writing on four equal criteria. Your weakest criterion usually caps how high you can go, even when other areas look strong.

This guide from **IELTS AI Tutor by IELTSGRADER** walks through each criterion, how the overall band is calculated, and how to use criterion-level feedback to plan practice. It is written for Academic and General Training candidates who want a clear map, not a vague pep talk.

## Why the four criteria matter more than one number

An overall 6.5 can hide very different profiles:

- Task Response 7, Coherence 7, Lexical 6, Grammar 6 → vocabulary and accuracy are the bottleneck
- Task Response 6, Coherence 6, Lexical 7, Grammar 7 → ideas and structure need work first

Two students with the same overall band need different study plans. ## The four criteria at a glance

| Criterion | Task 1 name | Task 2 name | Core question |
|-----------|-------------|-------------|----------------|
| Task | Task Achievement (TA) | Task Response (TR) | Did you fully answer the question? |
| Organisation | Coherence & Cohesion (CC) | Coherence & Cohesion | Is the writing easy to follow? |
| Vocabulary | Lexical Resource (LR) | Lexical Resource | Is word choice precise and varied? |
| Grammar | Grammatical Range & Accuracy (GRA) | Grammatical Range & Accuracy | Are sentences controlled and varied? |

Each criterion is scored from Band 0 to 9. Your Writing band is the average of the four, then rounded to the nearest half or whole band according to IELTS rounding rules.

## Task Achievement (Task 1) and Task Response (Task 2)

### Academic Task 1: Task Achievement

Examiners look for:

- A clear **overview** of the main trends or features (not a list of every number)
- Accurate selection of key data
- Comparison where relevant
- Coverage of the visual without inventing causes or opinions

A common Band 6 pattern: you describe some data correctly but miss a clear overview, or you dump every figure without highlighting what matters. Band 7 typically shows a purposeful overview and selective, accurate detail.

### General Training Task 1: Task Achievement

For letters, Task Achievement means:

- Covering all bullet points in the prompt
- Using an appropriate tone (formal, semi-formal, or informal)
- Making the purpose of the letter clear from the start

Missing one bullet or mixing casual slang into a formal complaint letter often keeps TA at 6 even when grammar looks fine. See our [GT formal letter checklist](/blog/gt-formal-letter-checklist) for a practical audit.

### Task 2: Task Response

For essays, Task Response asks:

- Did you address every part of the question?
- Is your position clear throughout (especially for opinion and discuss-both-views prompts)?
- Are ideas developed with explanation and examples, not just stated?

Band 6 essays often have a clear opinion but thin development, short paragraphs that repeat the same idea. Band 7 essays extend ideas with specific support. Compare patterns in [Band 6 vs Band 7 Task 2](/blog/band-6-vs-7-task-2).

## Coherence & Cohesion

This criterion is about **how** ideas are organised, not how fancy your linking words sound.

Examiners notice:

- Logical paragraphing (one main idea per body paragraph)
- Clear progression from introduction to conclusion
- Referencing (this, these, such) that connects ideas without confusion
- Linking devices used accurately, not a chain of “Moreover… Furthermore… In addition…” with no real connection

Overusing memorised connectors is a classic Band 6 habit. Under-paragraphing (one long block) or jumping topics mid-paragraph also hurts CC. A focused [14-day Coherence & Cohesion plan](/blog/coherence-cohesion-14-day-plan) can fix this faster than memorising more transition phrases.

## Lexical Resource

Lexical Resource rewards:

- Range appropriate to the topic
- Precision (the right word, not the rarest word)
- Collocations that sound natural
- Awareness of style (academic vs letter tone)

What does **not** automatically raise your band: stuffing uncommon synonyms that do not fit the context. “Ameliorate the predicament of educational institutions” is usually worse than “improve schools” if the rest of the sentence is awkward.

Band 6 writers often repeat the same nouns and verbs. Band 7 writers paraphrase key ideas and use topic vocabulary with fewer word-choice errors. For Task 1 trend language, see [trends vocabulary](/blog/task-1-trends-vocabulary). For the Band 6→7 vocabulary jump, see [lexical resource collocations](/blog/lexical-resource-band-6-to-7).

## Grammatical Range & Accuracy

GRA balances two things:

1. **Range**, simple and complex structures (conditionals, relative clauses, passive where useful)
2. **Accuracy**, errors that do not block meaning vs errors that confuse the reader

Frequent small errors (articles, subject–verb agreement, tense) can keep you at 6 even if you attempt complex sentences. Occasional slips with mostly controlled complex grammar is more typical of Band 7.

A practical rule from tutoring: fix the error types that appear in every essay before chasing exotic structures. Your AI tutor flags recurring grammar patterns so you can target them in rewrites.

## How the overall Writing band is calculated

1. Score each of the four criteria (e.g. TR 6, CC 7, LR 6, GRA 7).
2. Average them: (6 + 7 + 6 + 7) ÷ 4 = 6.5.
3. Apply IELTS rounding: averages ending in .25 round down to the nearest half band; .75 round up to the next whole band. (Example: 6.25 → 6.0; 6.75 → 7.0.)

Because the average is equal-weighted, raising your lowest criterion by 0.5 often moves the overall band more than polishing an already-strong area.

## Academic vs General Training: same criteria, different tasks

Both modules use the same four criteria and the same overall calculation. The difference is the Task 1 product:

- Academic: charts, graphs, maps, processes
- General Training: letters

Task 2 is an essay in both modules, with similar TR expectations. If you are choosing a path, read [Academic vs General Training Writing](/blog/academic-vs-general-training-writing).

## Common myths about Writing scores

**“I need Band 7 vocabulary to get Band 7.”**  
Not alone. Unclear Task Response or weak paragraphing can hold you at 6.5 even with good words.

**“Longer essays always score higher.”**  
Length helps only if ideas are developed. Padding with repetition can hurt Coherence and Lexical Resource.

**“Memorised templates guarantee Band 7.”**  
Templates that ignore the question damage Task Response. Examiners and tutors both notice off-topic or generic content.

**“Grammar mistakes mean I cannot get 7.”**  
Some Band 7 scripts still have occasional errors. Systematic errors that confuse meaning are the bigger problem.

## Frequently asked questions

### What are the four IELTS Writing criteria?

Task Response/Achievement, Coherence & Cohesion, Lexical Resource, and Grammatical Range & Accuracy.

### How is the overall Writing band calculated?

Criteria are averaged and rounded to the nearest half band (practice tools approximate this).

### Is Task 2 worth more?

Yes — Task 2 contributes more to the Writing score than Task 1.
