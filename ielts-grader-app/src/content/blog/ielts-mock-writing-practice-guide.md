# How to Practice IELTS Writing Like the Real Exam

Untimed essays teach ideas. **Mock writing tests** teach performance. If your practice never matches exam pressure, your test-day band can disappoint even when your notebook work looks fine.

This guide from **IELTS AI Tutor by IELTSGRADER** shows how to run realistic Writing mocks, review them properly, and turn each report into the next week’s plan.

## Why mock conditions matter

Under the real exam you must:

- Complete Task 1 (20 minutes recommended) and Task 2 (40 minutes recommended)
- Meet minimum word counts without endless polishing
- Handle an unfamiliar prompt
- Keep handwriting or typing clear enough to be read

Practising only “perfect” untimed essays hides timing and decision problems. A proper mock surfaces them early.

Use the [IELTS mock writing test](/ielts-mock-writing-test) when you want a productised timed flow, or self-time with the [essay checker](/ielts-essay-checker) afterward.

## Exam timing blueprint

| Task | Suggested time | Minimum words | Priority tip |
|------|----------------|---------------|--------------|
| Task 1 (Academic chart or GT letter) | 20 minutes | 150 | Overview / all bullets first |
| Task 2 (Essay) | 40 minutes | 250 | Task Response depth over fancy vocab |
| Total | 60 minutes |, | Do not steal Task 2 time for Task 1 perfection |

Some candidates write Task 2 first. That can work if you still protect Task 1 quality. Pick one order and practise it consistently so test day feels automatic.

## How often to mock

| Stage | Mock frequency | Focus |
|-------|----------------|-------|
| 8+ weeks out | Every 2 weeks | Diagnose weak criteria |
| 4–8 weeks out | Weekly | Timing + rewrite habits |
| Final 2 weeks | 2 mocks + lighter drills | Stability, not new tricks |

Between mocks, do targeted drills (coherence plans, trend vocabulary, letter checklists). Mocks without deliberate practice become score collecting.

## A complete mock-day routine

### Before (10 minutes)

- Clear desk; phone on silent
- Prepare paper or clean document
- Decide Academic vs GT materials
- Set two timers (20 + 40) or one 60-minute timer with a midpoint alarm

### During (60 minutes)

1. Read Task 1 carefully; underline units/bullets.  
2. Plan 2–3 minutes (overview or letter purpose + bullets).  
3. Write Task 1; stop at ~20 minutes even if imperfect.  
4. Plan Task 2 (4–5 minutes): position + two body ideas.  
5. Write Task 2; reserve 3 minutes to check opinion consistency and grammar hotspots.

### After (30–45 minutes), the part people skip

1. Submit both tasks for feedback ([mock writing test](/ielts-mock-writing-test) flow or separate checks).  
2. Record four criterion scores for each task.  
3. Choose **one** priority criterion for the next 7 days.  
4. Rewrite the weaker task once within 48 hours.  
5. Store the report so you can compare the next mock.

Without step 3–4, mocks do not improve bands. See [feedback to study plan](/blog/feedback-to-study-plan).

## What to measure besides overall band

Track a simple mock log:

| Date | T1 TA/CC/LR/GRA | T2 TR/CC/LR/GRA | Timing ok? | Priority next week |
|------|-----------------|-----------------|------------|--------------------|
| | | | Y/N | |

Overall band noise can hide progress. Criterion trends tell the truth, especially if you are on a [6.5 plateau](/blog/stuck-at-band-6-5-plateau).

## Academic vs GT mock setup

**Academic:** one visual Task 1 + one Task 2 essay. Practise overviews using [trends vocabulary](/blog/task-1-trends-vocabulary) and models like the [bar chart Band 7 sample](/blog/task-1-bar-chart-band-7).

**General Training:** one letter + one Task 2 essay. Use the [formal letter checklist](/blog/gt-formal-letter-checklist) and [Band 8 complaint sample](/blog/gt-letter-sample-band-8-complaint).

Module differences: [Academic vs GT writing](/blog/academic-vs-general-training-writing).

## Common mock mistakes

1. **Pausing the timer to look up words**, breaks realism  
2. **Skipping Task 1 overview / letter bullets**, free TA losses  
3. **Writing Task 2 with no plan**, coherence collapses under pressure  
4. **Checking the score and moving on**, no rewrite, no learning  
5. **Changing strategy every mock**, never stabilises timing  
6. **Only mocking Task 2**: Task 1 still counts toward Writing  

## After the report: a 7-day mini sprint

Example: mock shows Task 2 Coherence 6, other criteria 7.

- Days 1–2: topic sentence drills  
- Days 3–4: one-idea-per-paragraph rewrites  
- Day 5: timed Task 2 focusing on paragraph jobs  
- Day 6: rest or light vocabulary  
- Day 7: partial mock (Task 2 only) or full mock if weekly schedule says so  

For a fuller product journey, read [mock exam to 14-day sprint](/blog/mock-exam-to-14-day-sprint). For CC specifically, use the [14-day Coherence plan](/blog/coherence-cohesion-14-day-plan).

## Handwriting and computer-delivered notes

- **Paper-based:** practise legible handwriting; leave margins; if you scan work into a checker, follow [OCR tips](/blog/handwritten-essay-ocr-tips).  
- **Computer-delivered:** practise typing essays in one sitting; do not rely on endless backspacing in a way you will not have time for.

Match your practice medium to your registered test format when possible.
