# Lexical Resource Band 6 to 7: Collocations That Sound Natural

The Lexical Resource jump from Band 6 to Band 7 is rarely about learning obscure words. It is about **collocations**, natural word partnerships, plus less repetition and fewer inaccurate “advanced” choices.

This guide from **IELTS AI Tutor by IELTSGRADER** gives you collocation banks, before/after upgrades, and a practice method you can run through the [essay checker](/ielts-essay-checker).

## What Band 7 Lexical Resource looks like

Typical Band 7 features:

- Flexible paraphrase of key ideas 
- Topic-appropriate collocations 
- Awareness of style (academic vs letter) 
- Errors that do not prevent meaning 

Typical Band 6 features:

- Adequate vocabulary with noticeable repetition 
- Occasional wrong word choice 
- Awkward attempts at rare synonyms 

See the wider band contrast in [Band 6 vs 7 Task 2](/blog/band-6-vs-7-task-2).

## Rule 1: Collocations beat synonyms

**Band 6:** *make a research* / *do a decision* / *big damage* 
**Band 7:** *conduct research* / *make a decision* / *cause significant damage*

Build pairs, not isolated words.

## High-utility academic collocations (Task 2)

### Education

- pursue higher education 
- widen access to… 
- tuition fees / financial barriers 
- curriculum design 
- academic achievement 

### Technology

- remote communication 
- digital literacy 
- automate routine tasks 
- data privacy concerns 
- screen time 

### Environment

- curb emissions 
- renewable energy 
- sustainable transport 
- environmental regulations 
- carbon footprint 

### Health / lifestyle

- sedentary lifestyle 
- preventive care 
- mental wellbeing 
- public health campaigns 
- healthcare access 

### Work / society

- job security 
- work–life balance 
- income inequality 
- social mobility 
- entry-level roles 

**Drill:** Pick one topic. Write 8 sentences using 8 collocations. No essay yet.

## Paraphrase frames that sound natural

Instead of repeating *important*:

- plays a crucial role in… 
- is a decisive factor in… 
- has a significant impact on… 

Instead of repeating *problem*:

- drawback / limitation / unintended consequence / structural barrier 

Instead of repeating *people say*:

- advocates argue that… 
- critics contend that… 
- it is often claimed that… 

Use two or three alternatives per essay, not twelve.

## Before / after upgrades

### Example 1

**Band 6:** Technology makes life easy and also makes life difficult for people. 
**Band 7:** Technology can remove friction from daily tasks, yet it may also fragment attention when notifications go unmanaged.

### Example 2

**Band 6:** The government should give money to poor students for university. 
**Band 7:** Governments can widen access through need-based grants and income-contingent loans.

### Example 3

**Band 6:** Pollution is increasing and this is bad for the environment. 
**Band 7:** Rising emissions continue to degrade air quality, particularly in dense urban corridors.

Same ideas; better partnerships.

## Task 1 collocations (Academic)

Pair with [trends vocabulary](/blog/task-1-trends-vocabulary):

- a sharp increase / a gradual decline 
- peaked at / bottomed out at 
- narrowed the gap / widened the gap 
- remained relatively stable 
- the most marked change 

Avoid drama words that overstate small changes.

## GT letter collocations (formal)

From [formal letter](/blog/gt-formal-letter-checklist) practice:

- I am writing to complain about… 
- cause considerable inconvenience 
- request a full refund 
- at your earliest convenience 
- unresolved issue 

Tone precision is Lexical Resource as well as Task Achievement.

## The anti-thesaurus rule

If you cannot use a word correctly in an original sentence, do not force it into the exam.

Risky Band 6 habit: *ameliorate*, *plethora*, *myriad*, *paramount* in every paragraph. 
Safer Band 7 habit: precise verbs and nouns that fit.

## How your AI tutor flags LR issues

[IELTS AI Tutor](/ielts-ai-tutor) often highlights:

- Repetition loops 
- Awkward word choice 
- Informal lexis in academic essays 
- Degree mismatches in Task 1 

Those map to LR items in the [error taxonomy](/blog/ielts-writing-error-taxonomy). Fix cards should become bank entries, not one-time edits.

## Collocation study method that sticks

1. Notice a natural phrase in a model essay ([Band 7 technology](/blog/task-2-sample-band-7-technology)). 
2. Write it in a notebook with one original sentence. 
3. Reuse it in a new essay within 48 hours. 
4. If feedback marks it wrong, correct the bank. 

Passive reading lists do not raise Lexical Resource. Retrieval does.

## Common LR myths

**“I need Band 9 words for Band 7.”** False. 
**“Longer words score higher.”** False. 
**“Never repeat any word.”** False, forced paraphrase can sound unnatural. 
**“Idioms always help.”** Risky in Academic essays; many idioms feel informal.

## Topic family practice (two weeks)

Pick one family only (education, environment, work, or technology):

- Days 1–3: collect 25 collocations from trusted models and your own corrected sentences 
- Days 4–7: two timed essays in that family only 
- Days 8–10: rewrite the weaker essay focusing on repetition removal 
- Days 11–14: one new timed essay + one Task 1/letter day so other skills do not decay 

Staying inside one family accelerates Lexical Resource because retrieval repeats. Jumping topics every day resets the bank.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I improve Lexical Resource from 6 to 7?

Prioritise precise collocations and natural phrasing over rare words used incorrectly.

### Do idioms help LR in Task 2?

Conversational idioms often hurt register. Prefer formal collocations.

### Should I memorise synonym lists?

Only in context sentences. Isolated synonym swaps create collocation errors.
