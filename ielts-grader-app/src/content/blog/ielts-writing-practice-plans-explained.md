# IELTS Writing Practice Plans Explained: Free, Weekly, Monthly

Practice plans should match how you learn — not how loud an advert is. **IELTS AI Tutor by IELTSGRADER** offers different intensities of Writing tutoring so you can diagnose once, sprint weekly, or train consistently for a month. This educational guide explains the logic behind free vs weekly vs monthly practice so you can choose calmly.

Always confirm current details on [pricing](/pricing). Plan names and limits can evolve; the learning design below is what matters.

## The educational idea (not a hard sell)

Writing improves through cycles:

**Diagnose → focus → practise → rewrite → retest**

Different plan intensities simply change how many cycles you can complete. See also [free vs paid checker](/blog/free-vs-paid-ielts-checker) and [feedback to study plan](/blog/feedback-to-study-plan).

## Free: diagnosis and format learning

### Best for

- First-time users  
- Understanding criterion feedback  
- Deciding whether the tutor style fits you  
- Baseline “what is my weakest criterion?”  

### Learning outcomes

- See Task Response/Achievement, Coherence, Lexical Resource, Grammar notes  
- Experience fix-card style guidance  
- Learn how to read a report without panic  

### How to use free well

1. Submit one honest timed task via the [essay checker](/ielts-essay-checker) or [Task 2 checker](/ielts-task-2-checker).  
2. Write a 7-day drill plan for the lowest criterion.  
3. Rewrite once even if you cannot check again immediately.  

Free is a doorway, not a full course.

## Weekly: sprint rhythm

### Best for

- Candidates who can write 2+ times per week  
- Test dates roughly within 1–2 months  
- People who need accountability structure without a long commitment  

### Learning outcomes

- Maintain a mock-or-check → sprint loop  
- Fix one criterion family per week  
- Keep timing skills warm  

### Suggested weekly shape

| Day pattern | Activity |
|-------------|----------|
| Start | Timed task + report |
| Mid-week | Drills + rewrite |
| End | Second timed task or partial mock |

Align with [mock to 14-day sprint](/blog/mock-exam-to-14-day-sprint) by treating two weeks as one theme when needed.

### Weekly is wasted if…

- You only submit and never rewrite  
- You change focus every check  
- You ignore Task 1/letters for your module  

## Monthly: consistency and pattern tracking

### Best for

- Candidates building toward a target band over several weeks  
- Plateau breakers who need repeated evidence ([6.5 plateau](/blog/stuck-at-band-6-5-plateau))  
- Learners who want [learning editions](/blog/personalized-learning-editions-guide) powered by multiple scripts  

### Learning outcomes

- Complete multiple full cycles  
- See whether criterion trends actually move  
- Combine mocks, drills, and editions  

### Suggested monthly arc

| Week | Focus |
|------|--------|
| 1 | Baseline mock + theme selection |
| 2 | Theme sprint + mid-checks |
| 3 | Second theme or deepen same if still weak |
| 4 | Full mock comparison + next-month plan |

Mocks: [mock writing test](/ielts-mock-writing-test). Mock habits: [mock practice guide](/blog/ielts-mock-writing-practice-guide).

## Choosing with a simple decision grid

| Situation | Start with |
|-----------|------------|
| Never seen criterion feedback | Free |
| Can practise intensely for 7–14 days | Weekly-style |
| Preparing across 3–6+ weeks | Monthly-style |
| Unsure you will open the app twice | Free + calendar reminder; upgrade when habit exists |
| Need Speaking coaching too | Writing plan + separate human Speaking help ([AI vs human](/blog/ai-tutor-vs-human-tutor)) |

## Sample student paths (illustrative)

### Path A — Busy professional, test in 5 weeks

Week 0: free diagnosis.  
Weeks 1–4: weekly sprint intensity; one mock per week; Coherence then Task Response themes.

### Path B — Student with 3 months

Month 1: monthly consistency; build Task 1 + Task 2 balance ([Academic vs GT](/blog/academic-vs-general-training-writing)).  
Month 2: learning edition on recurring LR/CC issues.  
Month 3: mock-heavy taper.

### Path C — Budget-first

Free check → 14 days self-drills using blog plans → paid burst for two weeks of checks before the exam.

## Ethics: what we will not claim

This guide does not invent student counts, guaranteed bands, or fake ratings. Plans buy **practice infrastructure**. Your writing hours still do the improvement.

## What “weekly” and “monthly” mean in study terms

Think in completed feedback cycles, not calendar labels alone.

A useful weekly cycle includes at least:

- One timed task with a full criterion report  
- One rewrite addressing the top fix theme  
- One drill session that is not a full essay  
- A short log of whether the target criterion moved  

A useful monthly arc includes at least:

- Two full mocks (start and end)  
- Two focused sprints (or one deep 14-day theme)  
- Enough checks to see a trend, not a single lucky spike  
- A decision point: continue the same criterion or switch  

If your paid access does not turn into those behaviours, the plan intensity is irrelevant. Habit design beats receipt design.

## When to downgrade intensity

Upgrade is not always the answer. Downgrade or pause paid intensity if:

- You are not rewriting  
- Life schedule cannot support two sessions a week  
- You need a human Speaking coach more urgently than more Writing checks  

You can return to free diagnosis later and rebuild. Educational pricing should follow capacity.
