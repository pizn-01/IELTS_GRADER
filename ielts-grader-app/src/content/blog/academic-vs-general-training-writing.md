# Academic vs General Training Writing: Which Path and How to Practise

IELTS Writing is not identical for every candidate. **Academic** and **General Training (GT)** share Task 2 essay skills and the same four scoring criteria, but Task 1 is a different genre. Choosing the wrong practice materials wastes weeks.

This comparison from **IELTS AI Tutor by IELTSGRADER** clarifies the differences and how to train for your module.

## Quick comparison

| Feature | Academic | General Training |
|---------|----------|------------------|
| Task 1 | Describe a visual (graph, chart, table, diagram, map) | Write a letter (formal, semi-formal, or informal) |
| Task 2 | Discursive essay | Discursive essay (often slightly more everyday topics) |
| Criteria | TA, CC, LR, GRA (Task 1); TR, CC, LR, GRA (Task 2) | Same structure |
| Timing | 60 minutes total | 60 minutes total |
| Word counts | 150+ / 250+ | 150+ / 250+ |

Which module you take depends on your institution, visa, or professional requirement, not on which feels easier.

## Task 1: the real fork in the road

### Academic Task 1 skills

- Reading axes and units correctly  
- Writing a clear overview  
- Selecting key features  
- Comparing data  
- Using trend and comparison language  

Guides: [trends vocabulary](/blog/task-1-trends-vocabulary), [bar chart Band 7 sample](/blog/task-1-bar-chart-band-7), [Task Response vs Achievement](/blog/task-response-vs-achievement).

### GT Task 1 skills

- Identifying tone from the recipient  
- Covering every bullet with enough detail  
- Stating purpose early  
- Using appropriate openings and sign-offs  

Guides: [formal letter checklist](/blog/gt-formal-letter-checklist), [Band 8 complaint letter](/blog/gt-letter-sample-band-8-complaint).

**Practical warning:** Practising only Academic charts will not prepare you for GT letters, and vice versa. Task Achievement fails differently in each.

## Task 2: mostly shared, with topic flavour

Both modules require:

- Clear Task Response  
- Organised paragraphs  
- Precise vocabulary  
- Controlled grammar  

GT Task 2 topics can feel more practical (work, community, daily life), while Academic topics may lean more abstract, but the essay skills transfer. Samples across bands help both audiences: [Band 6 education](/blog/task-2-sample-band-6-education), [Band 7 technology](/blog/task-2-sample-band-7-technology), [Band 7.5 opinion](/blog/task-2-opinion-essay-band-7-5).

## Scoring: same engine, different Task 1 evidence

Your Writing band averages four criteria. The names Task Achievement vs Task Response depend on the task, not the module brand. Full primer: [how IELTS writing is scored](/blog/how-ielts-writing-is-scored).

That means a GT candidate with perfect essays but incomplete letters can still be capped, and an Academic candidate with strong essays but no overview faces the same logic.

## Which module should you take?

You usually do **not** choose freely. Check:

- University / professional body requirements  
- Immigration guidance for your destination  
- The module on your registration confirmation  

If you truly have a choice, decide based on requirements and your strengths, not on blog myths that “GT is always easier.” GT letters demand precise task coverage; Academic visuals demand data literacy. Different hard.

## Practice plans by module

### Academic weekly mix

- 2× timed Task 1 charts  
- 2× timed Task 2  
- 1× rewrite  
- Every 1–2 weeks: full [mock](/ielts-mock-writing-test)  

### GT weekly mix

- 2× timed letters (vary formal/informal)  
- 2× timed Task 2  
- 1× rewrite  
- Every 1–2 weeks: full mock  

Both benefit from criterion-focused sprints ([mock to 14-day sprint](/blog/mock-exam-to-14-day-sprint)).

## Shared skills worth training together

Even across modules, these transfer:

- Paragraph coherence ([14-day CC plan](/blog/coherence-cohesion-14-day-plan))  
- Lexical precision ([LR Band 6→7](/blog/lexical-resource-band-6-to-7))  
- Grammar error logs ([error taxonomy](/blog/ielts-writing-error-taxonomy))  
- Timed performance habits ([mock practice guide](/blog/ielts-mock-writing-practice-guide))  

Do not duplicate effort: train shared skills once, module-specific Task 1 separately.

## Common module mistakes

1. Preparing Academic for months, then discovering registration is GT (or reverse)  
2. Ignoring Task 1 because Task 2 “counts more” emotionally (both count)  
3. Using informal letter tone for formal GT prompts  
4. Writing Task 1 Academic opinions (*This is because governments…*)  
5. Copying friends’ practice plans from a different module  

## Topic difficulty myths

“GT is easier” usually means “GT Task 1 feels more familiar than charts.” That familiarity disappears when a formal letter needs three detailed bullets and precise tone. Likewise, “Academic is harder” often means discomfort with data language, which improves quickly with [trends vocabulary](/blog/task-1-trends-vocabulary) drills.

Task 2 difficulty depends more on your idea development and coherence than on the module label. A GT candidate still needs Band 7 essay skills for a Band 7 Writing score.

## Examiner lens reminder

Examiners are not awarding “Academic points” or “GT points.” They award criterion bands. Your job is to give them the evidence each task requires:

- Academic Task 1 evidence = overview + key features + comparisons  
- GT Task 1 evidence = purpose + bullets + tone  
- Task 2 evidence = answered question + developed position  

Decode the naming in [TR vs TA](/blog/task-response-vs-achievement).

## Switching modules late

If you must switch:

- Spend the first week exclusively on the new Task 1 genre  
- Keep Task 2 practice running so essay fitness does not drop  
- Take a diagnostic mock in the new format immediately  
- Rebuild a 14-day sprint around the new Task 1 weakness ([mock to sprint](/blog/mock-exam-to-14-day-sprint))  

Learning editions can pivot to letter or chart patterns, [personalized learning editions](/blog/personalized-learning-editions-guide).
