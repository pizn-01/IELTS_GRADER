# Best Essay Structure for IELTS Writing Task 2

The best essay structure for IELTS Writing Task 2 is usually **four paragraphs**: introduction with a clear position, two focused body paragraphs, and a short conclusion. That map fits 40 minutes and supports Coherence without empty linker chains. Question type changes each body’s job — not whether you need a plan.

This guide from **IELTS AI Tutor by IELTSGRADER** gives a step framework, structures by question type, before/after excerpts, a Band 7 checklist, and a practice prompt you can score today.

## Step framework: plan before you write (5 minutes)

| Step | Time | Output |
|------|------|--------|
| 1. Identify the question type | 30–45 sec | Opinion / both views / advantages / problem-solution / two-part |
| 2. Decide your position | 30 sec | Agree / partly / outweigh / priority answer |
| 3. Assign paragraph jobs | 1 min | One job per body paragraph |
| 4. Add one example each | 1–2 min | Concrete, relevant support |
| 5. Check coverage | 30 sec | Every part of the question answered |

If you skip planning, structure collapses into one long block — a common Coherence cap. Related: [how many paragraphs](/blog/how-many-paragraphs-ielts-essay) and [how to generate ideas](/blog/how-to-generate-ideas-ielts-task-2).

## The core 4-paragraph structure (default)

1. **Introduction (2–3 sentences)** — paraphrase the topic + state your position (or outline for discuss-both-views).  
2. **Body 1** — strongest reason / first view — claim → explain → example.  
3. **Body 2** — second reason / other view + your stance — same pattern.  
4. **Conclusion (1–2 sentences)** — restate position; no new arguments.

**When five paragraphs help:** discuss-both-views with a separate short opinion body, or two-part questions that need two clean answers plus a brief wrap-up. Five random short paragraphs without development still score mid-band.

## Best structure by IELTS Task 2 question type

### 1. Agree / disagree (“To what extent…”)

| Paragraph | Job |
|-----------|-----|
| Intro | Paraphrase + clear extent (agree / partly / disagree) |
| Body 1 | Main reason for your side + example |
| Body 2 | Second reason **or** concession + why your side still wins |
| Conclusion | Restate extent |

Full model: [Band 7.5 opinion sample](/blog/task-2-opinion-essay-band-7-5).

### 2. Discuss both views + opinion

| Paragraph | Job |
|-----------|-----|
| Intro | Paraphrase + say you will discuss both and give your view |
| Body 1 | View A with fair development |
| Body 2 | View B + your opinion (or Body 2 = View B, Body 3 = opinion) |
| Conclusion | Clear final position |

Do not “sit on the fence” if the prompt asks for your opinion.

### 3. Advantages / disadvantages (or outweigh)

| Paragraph | Job |
|-----------|-----|
| Intro | Paraphrase + direct answer to “outweigh?” if asked |
| Body 1 | Advantages (or the heavier side) |
| Body 2 | Disadvantages + weighing decision |
| Conclusion | Confirm which side wins and why |

### 4. Problem / solution

| Paragraph | Job |
|-----------|-----|
| Intro | Paraphrase + preview that you will cover problems and solutions |
| Body 1 | 1–2 problems with causes |
| Body 2 | Matching solutions (not unrelated ideas) |
| Conclusion | Brief summary of the link problem → solution |

### 5. Two-part questions

| Paragraph | Job |
|-----------|-----|
| Intro | Paraphrase both questions |
| Body 1 | Answer question 1 fully |
| Body 2 | Answer question 2 fully |
| Conclusion | Short synthesis (optional if both bodies already close cleanly) |

Missing one part is a Task Response failure no stylish vocabulary can fix. See [Task Response vs Achievement](/blog/task-response-vs-achievement).

## Body paragraph formula (claim → explain → example)

Every strong body paragraph does three jobs in order:

1. **Claim** — topic sentence that states the paragraph’s single idea.  
2. **Explain** — why the claim matters for the question (*because…*).  
3. **Example** — a concrete illustration (school, workplace, city policy — not a private diary dump).  
4. **Optional mini-link** — one sentence tying back to your overall position.

### Before (claim only)

> Online shopping is convenient for busy people and it is popular.

### After (full formula)

> Online shopping is convenient for workers with long hours because deliveries remove the need to travel during peak traffic. For example, a nurse finishing a late shift can order household goods without visiting a crowded supermarket. That time saving is why convenience often outweighs the loss of in-store advice for many households.

Same idea; Task Response depth rises because the paragraph *develops*. Practise this formula twice per essay — that is usually enough for Band 7 structure when the question is fully answered.

## Introduction and conclusion: keep them short

**Introduction traps**

- Three-sentence paraphrase that never states a position  
- Memorised opener that does not match *this* question  
- Announcing “I will discuss advantages and disadvantages” when the prompt asked “to what extent do you agree”

**Conclusion traps**

- New arguments that belong in a body paragraph  
- Copy-pasting the introduction with synonyms  
- Hedging into “it depends” after promising a clear opinion

A clean pair: intro locks the stance; conclusion echoes it in one or two sentences. Leave word count for the bodies.

## Before / after: structure that looks busy vs structure that scores

**Prompt:** Some people think children should learn a foreign language in primary school. Others think it should wait until secondary school. Discuss both views and give your opinion.

### Weak structure (Band 6 Coherence pattern)

> Primary is better because kids learn fast and secondary students are busy and also parents want early classes moreover teachers say children get confused so I think early is good for jobs and travel and hospitals need languages too.

One block, mixed jobs, no paragraph map. Linkers cannot save this.

### Stronger structure (Band 7 pattern)

**Intro:** paraphrase + “I will examine both views; I favour an early start if lessons stay light.”

**Body 1 (early start):** natural acquisition + example (songs/games) + limit (must not crowd literacy).

**Body 2 (later start + opinion):** secondary focus can protect reading/maths; however, delayed start often treats language as exam-only — so I support early, light exposure.

**Conclusion:** restate preference + condition.

Same topic knowledge; clearer **paragraph jobs**. That is the real “best structure.”

## How structure maps to the four criteria

| Criterion | What good structure does |
|-----------|--------------------------|
| Task Response | Every question part gets a home; position stays visible |
| Coherence & Cohesion | One idea per paragraph; logical progression |
| Lexical Resource | Room for precise words instead of linker spam |
| Grammar | Shorter planned paragraphs → fewer collapsed sentences |

## Band 7 structure checklist (use before you submit)

- [ ] I named the question type correctly  
- [ ] My introduction states a usable position or clear outline  
- [ ] Each body paragraph has **one** job in the topic sentence  
- [ ] Every body has explanation + at least one concrete example  
- [ ] I answered every part of the prompt  
- [ ] Conclusion restates — it does not invent a new argument  
- [ ] Word count is at least 250 (aim 260–290) — [word count guide](/blog/how-many-words-ielts-task-2)  
- [ ] I did not paste a memorised intro that ignores *this* question  

If overall score stalls while ideas feel “enough,” structure/coherence is often the cap: [stuck at 6.5](/blog/stuck-at-band-6-5-plateau).

## Common structure myths

1. **“More paragraphs = higher band.”** No — undeveloped fragments hurt Coherence.  
2. **“I must use Firstly / Moreover / Furthermore.”** Mechanical linkers without logic stay Band 6.  
3. **“Balanced essays always win.”** Not when the prompt demands a clear opinion.  
4. **“Templates guarantee Band 7.”** Templates that miss the question lower Task Response.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best essay structure for IELTS Writing Task 2?

Four paragraphs is the most reliable default: introduction, two bodies, conclusion. Adjust body jobs to the question type; use five paragraphs only when a third body has a clear, necessary job.

### How many body paragraphs should I write?

Two is enough for most prompts if both are developed. Three bodies can work for discuss-both-views + opinion, but only with real development — not one-sentence paragraphs.

### Should I memorise one template for all Task 2 essays?

Memorise a **planning method** and paragraph jobs, not a fixed essay. Question types need different body missions.

### Does a good structure raise Lexical Resource?

Indirectly. Clear paragraphs free attention for precise vocabulary instead of filler linkers and repetition.
