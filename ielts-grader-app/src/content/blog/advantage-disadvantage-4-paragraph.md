# Advantage Disadvantage Essay: 4-Paragraph Formula

Advantages/disadvantages prompts come in two sub-types that need different conclusions: a plain "what are the advantages and disadvantages" (balanced answer expected) and an "outweigh" version (weighing decision expected). Mixing the two up is a quiet Task Response error that this formula from **IELTS AI Tutor by IELTSGRADER** is built to prevent.

## The unique practice prompt for this template

**Prompt:** Many city governments are actively promoting tourism to boost their local economies. Do the advantages of this trend outweigh the disadvantages?

Every worked example below is built around this city-tourism prompt so you can see the outweigh sub-type applied to one specific, current topic.

## Outweigh vs balanced: two different tasks

| Sub-type | Example wording | Conclusion job |
|----------|------------------|-----------------|
| Balanced | "What are the advantages and disadvantages of…?" | Summarise both sides; a clear personal lean is optional but often still expected in practice writing |
| Outweigh | "Do the advantages outweigh the disadvantages?" | State and justify which side wins — this is a required, direct answer, not optional |

Treating an outweigh prompt as though it only wanted a list is one of the most common Task Response gaps in this essay type, because the question is really asking for a judgement, not just information.

## The 4-paragraph formula

| Paragraph | Job |
|-----------|-----|
| Introduction | Paraphrase the topic; if it is an outweigh prompt, state your weighing decision immediately |
| Body 1 | Advantages (or the heavier side, if you already know which wins) |
| Body 2 | Disadvantages (or the lighter side) plus your weighing explanation |
| Conclusion | Confirm which side wins and briefly say why, or summarise both fairly for a balanced prompt |

This is the same 4-paragraph default used across all Task 2 types — see the [full map](/blog/task-2-essay-types-hub#the-4-paragraph-default-map) for how it adapts elsewhere.

## Where your position belongs in the introduction

For outweigh prompts, state your weighing decision in the introduction, not just the conclusion. Waiting until the conclusion to reveal your judgement can make the two body paragraphs read like an undirected list rather than an argument building toward a decision.

**Introduction without an early position (weaker):**

> Tourism is increasingly promoted by city governments around the world. This essay will discuss the advantages and disadvantages of this trend.

**Introduction with an early, specific position (stronger):**

> Tourism is increasingly promoted by city governments around the world, and in my view the economic advantages of this trend generally outweigh its disadvantages, provided the resulting revenue is reinvested in the infrastructure that supports it.

The second version signals from sentence one that body 2 will need to explain *why* the advantages still win despite the disadvantages it will raise — which sets up a more coherent essay.

## Mini sample: both body paragraphs

**Body 1 (advantages — the heavier side):**

> Tourism promotion generates direct economic benefits that ripple through a local economy far beyond hotels and airlines. Small businesses such as restaurants, tour operators, and craft retailers often see substantial revenue increases, and the tax income from this activity can fund public services that residents also use. A city that successfully attracts steady tourist numbers, for example, can often justify improved public transport that locals ride daily, not only visitors.

**Body 2 (disadvantages plus weighing explanation):**

> The disadvantages are real but more manageable than the gains. Popular destinations can suffer overcrowding, rising housing costs as short-term rentals displace long-term residents, and strain on local infrastructure during peak seasons. However, these problems typically respond to policy tools — rental caps, staggered ticketing for major sites, and reinvested tourist taxes — whereas the economic benefits of tourism are harder to replace with an equally scalable alternative. For that reason, the advantages still outweigh the disadvantages when a city manages growth deliberately rather than passively.

## Common advantages/disadvantages traps

- **Treating "outweigh" as "list both sides"** and never actually stating which side wins.
- **Introducing your weighing decision only in the conclusion**, making the two bodies feel unplanned.
- **Unequal development**, where advantages get three sentences and disadvantages get one — even the "losing" side needs a real reason and example.
- **Confusing this type with discuss-both-views**, which compares two *opinions* about a topic rather than the pros and cons of one thing; see [discuss both views essay structure](/blog/discuss-both-views-structure) for the difference.

## Quick self-check before you submit

Run through these four checks in the last two minutes before finishing:

1. **Did you spot the exact wording?** Circle the words "outweigh," "advantages and disadvantages," or similar in the prompt itself, and confirm your conclusion matches what that wording actually demands.
2. **Is your weighing decision visible early**, not only in the conclusion, for outweigh-style prompts? A reader should be able to predict your conclusion by the end of the introduction.
3. **Does each side get a real reason and example**, even the side you consider weaker? A one-sentence "losing" side signals the paragraph was never fully planned.
4. **Does your conclusion actually decide**, rather than repeating "both sides have points" without a judgement, for outweigh prompts specifically?

## Why the weighing decision matters more than list length

Some candidates assume that naming more advantages than disadvantages (or vice versa) is itself a form of "outweighing." Examiners do not count list items; they read whether your reasoning explains *why* one side matters more, even when both sides are real. A short list of two well-explained advantages against two well-explained disadvantages, followed by a paragraph that explicitly compares their relative importance, outscores a longer list of four thin advantages and one thin disadvantage with no real comparison at the end. Development and explicit comparison, not volume, are what Task Response is checking for in this essay type.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the difference between "advantages and disadvantages" and "outweigh" prompts?

A plain advantages/disadvantages prompt expects both sides summarised fairly. An outweigh prompt is a direct question that requires you to state and justify which side wins — treating it as a simple list under-answers the task.

### Where should I state my position for an outweigh essay?

In the introduction, so both body paragraphs build toward a decision the reader already expects, then confirm that decision again in the conclusion.

### Do I need equal numbers of advantages and disadvantages?

No, but whichever side you cover needs genuine development — a reason plus an example — not just a longer list of short, undeveloped points. A single, fully developed disadvantage can carry a paragraph just as well as two thin ones, so long as the reasoning is complete.

### Is this the same as a problem-solution essay?

No. Advantages/disadvantages weighs the pros and cons of a trend or practice; problem/solution focuses on causes of a specific problem and matching remedies. See [problem solution essay structure](/blog/problem-solution-essay-structure) for the contrast.

### Can I give a personal opinion in a balanced advantages/disadvantages essay?

Yes, a brief personal lean in the conclusion is common and often strengthens Task Response, as long as the body paragraphs still developed both sides fairly first. Keep it short, though — a balanced prompt does not require the same depth of personal argument that an extent or discuss-both-views essay does.
