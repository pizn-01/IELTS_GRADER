# Can I Use I in IELTS Academic Writing Task 2?

Yes. In IELTS Academic Writing Task 2 you **can use “I”** (and *my*, *me*) when the question asks for your opinion. Phrases like *I believe*, *I agree*, and *In my view* are acceptable and often help Task Response because your position is unmistakable. What examiners penalise is an unclear stance — not the pronoun itself.

This guide from **IELTS AI Tutor by IELTSGRADER** clarifies the Academic vs GT myth, shows Band 6 vs Band 7 opinion language, and explains when impersonal style is better.

## Can I use "I" in IELTS Academic Writing Task 2? (rules)

| Situation | Use “I”? | Why |
|-----------|----------|-----|
| Agree/disagree / “to what extent” / “is this a positive development” | Yes | Position must be clear |
| Discuss both views and give your opinion | Yes (in opinion section) | Examiners need *your* view |
| Advantages outweigh disadvantages | Yes | You must weigh and decide |
| Purely descriptive Academic Task 1 (charts) | Avoid | Report data; no personal opinion |
| GT formal letter | Sometimes | Match tone; *I am writing to…* is normal |

The myth “never use I in Academic IELTS” comes from university essay advice, not from IELTS Task 2 band descriptors. Task Response rewards a **clear position throughout**.

## Why clear “I” statements help Task Response

Band 6 scripts often hide the opinion:

> Free transport has benefits and drawbacks. Public transport can reduce pollution. Cars are convenient. There are arguments on both sides.

No decision. For “to what extent do you agree,” that under-answers the task.

Band 7 scripts commit:

> I partly agree that city centres should restrict private cars, because cleaner air and safer streets outweigh the short-term inconvenience for drivers — provided cities expand reliable buses and cycling routes first.

“I partly agree” + conditions = clear Task Response. See more patterns in [Band 6 vs Band 7](/blog/band-6-vs-7-task-2) and [opinion essay Band 7.5](/blog/task-2-opinion-essay-band-7-5).

## Before / after: hidden opinion vs clear stance

**Prompt:** Some people think unpaid community service should be a compulsory part of high school. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

### Unclear (Task Response risk)

> Community service teaches responsibility. Schools already have many subjects. Parents may support volunteering. It depends on the situation.

### Clear with “I”

> I disagree that unpaid community service should be compulsory for every student. While voluntary projects can build empathy, forcing unpaid labour onto teenagers who already face exam pressure risks resentment and shallow participation. Schools should offer strong optional programmes with real mentoring instead of a blanket mandate.

Same topic knowledge; clearer position. The pronoun is not the magic — the **decision** is.

## Safer opinion frames (rotate these)

- *I agree / disagree that…*
- *I partly agree…*
- *In my view…*
- *I would argue that…*
- Impersonal alternative: *This essay argues that…* (also fine)

Do not write *I* twenty times. Use it to lock the stance in the introduction and conclusion, and optionally once in a body topic sentence.

## When to avoid “I”

- **Academic Task 1:** describe trends, do not say *I think the chart shows…*
- **Over-personal stories:** *I remember when my uncle…* as your only evidence — weak development
- **GT formal complaints:** use *I* for purpose (*I am writing to complain…*) but keep claims factual

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I use "I" in IELTS Academic Writing Task 2?

Yes. Opinion questions expect a personal position. *I agree* and *In my view* are acceptable academic Task 2 language.

### Will using "I" lower my band?

Not if your grammar is controlled and your position is relevant. Unclear or contradictory opinions lower Task Response — not the pronoun.

### Should I avoid "I" to sound more formal?

You can use impersonal phrasing (*This essay argues…*), but formality is not improved by hiding your answer. Clarity beats fake objectivity.

### Can I use "I" in Task 1 Academic?

Avoid opinion language. Describe what the data show. *Overall, X was higher than Y* is safer than *I can see that…*.

### How many times should I write "I"?

A few times is enough: introduction position, maybe one body reminder, conclusion restatement. Do not turn the essay into a diary.
