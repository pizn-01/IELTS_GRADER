# Academic Task 1: Describe Trends Without Repeating Vocabulary

In Academic Task 1, candidates often lose Lexical Resource marks for one simple reason: they write *increase* and *decrease* eight times. Examiners have read thousands of scripts; repetition stands out.

This guide from **IELTS AI Tutor by IELTSGRADER** gives you precise trend language, example sentences, and a practice method so your overview and details sound controlled — not copy-pasted from a memorised list.

## What “good” trend vocabulary actually means

Band 7 Lexical Resource in Task 1 is not about rare words. It is about:

- Accurate verbs and nouns for direction and degree
- Variety without forcing awkward synonyms
- Collocations that match the data (*rose sharply*, not *rose sharplyly*)
- Paraphrasing the rubric (*the chart shows* → *the graph illustrates*)

If you need a full model, see the [Band 7 bar chart sample](/blog/task-1-bar-chart-band-7).

## 1. Verbs for upward movement

| Neutral | Stronger / more precise | Example |
|---------|-------------------------|---------|
| increase | rise, grow, climb | Sales **rose** from 20 to 45.
| go up | edge up, inch up | Prices **edged up** by 2%.
| jump | surge, soar | Demand **surged** after 2018.
| recover | rebound | After a dip, figures **rebounded** in 2020.

**Caution:** *Soar* and *surge* need a large change. Do not use them for a 3% rise.

## 2. Verbs for downward movement

| Neutral | Stronger / more precise | Example |
|---------|-------------------------|---------|
| decrease | fall, drop, decline | Unemployment **fell** to 4%.
| go down | dip, slip | Output **dipped** slightly in May.
| drop sharply | plunge, plummet | Investment **plummeted** during the crisis.
| reduce | shrink (for size/amount) | The gap **shrank** over the decade.

**Caution:** *Plummet* implies a dramatic fall. Match the visual.

## 3. Verbs for stability and fluctuation

- **remain stable / remain steady / stay constant** — little change
- **level off / plateau** — rise or fall then flatten
- **fluctuate** — repeated ups and downs
- **vary** — differences between categories (not always a time trend)
- **hover around** — stay near a value with small movement

**Example:** After climbing to 60%, the proportion **levelled off** for the next three years.

## 4. Noun forms (essential for variety)

Mixing verbs and nouns lifts Lexical Resource:

- a rise / an increase / growth / an upward trend
- a fall / a decrease / a decline / a drop
- a fluctuation / a variation
- a peak / a low point / a high of X
- stability / a plateau

**Example:** The chart shows a steady **rise** in remote workers, followed by a brief **decline** in 2021.

## 5. Adverbs and adjectives for degree

| Small change | Moderate | Large / sudden |
|--------------|----------|----------------|
| slightly, marginally, gradually | steadily, moderately, significantly | sharply, dramatically, steeply, rapidly |

**Adjective forms:** a slight rise, a gradual decline, a sharp increase, a dramatic fall.

**Example:** Energy use increased **only marginally**, whereas transport emissions fell **sharply**.

## 6. Preposition patterns that sound natural

- rose **from** 10 **to** 25
- increased **by** 15 percentage points (not always “by 15%” if the chart shows points)
- peaked **at** 80 in 2019
- fell **to** a low of 12
- remained **at** around 30
- fluctuated **between** 40 and 55

Accuracy here also supports Task Achievement.

## 7. Comparison language (often missing at Band 6)

Trend vocabulary alone is not enough. Compare:

- **while / whereas** — X rose, whereas Y fell
- **compared with / relative to**
- **the largest increase** / **the most marked decline**
- **twice as high as** / **three times higher than** (check the data)
- **narrowed / widened** (for gaps)

**Example:** Home broadband subscriptions rose steadily, **whereas** public phone booth usage declined throughout the period.

## 8. Overview phrases that avoid “increase/decrease” spam

Your overview should summarise main trends without listing every number:

- Overall, X showed an upward trend, while Y declined.
- The most striking feature is the sharp growth in…
- X and Y followed similar patterns until 2015, after which their paths diverged.
- Despite some fluctuation, the general trend was upward.

Save detailed figures for body paragraphs.

## 9. Topic-neutral sentence frames

Use these frames and swap in accurate verbs:

1. *X rose steadily from A to B between YEAR and YEAR.*
2. *After peaking at A in YEAR, X fell back to B.*
3. *X remained relatively stable, hovering around A.*
4. *In contrast, Y experienced a sharp decline over the same period.*
5. *The gap between X and Y widened considerably after YEAR.*
6. *X overtook Y in YEAR, reaching a high of A.*

Practise by rewriting one chart description three ways using different frames.

## 10. Words to use carefully (or avoid)

| Risky choice | Why | Safer option |
|--------------|-----|--------------|
| skyrocket | often exaggerated | rose sharply / surged |
| nosedive | informal tone | fell sharply / plunged |
| crazy increase | informal | dramatic rise |
| went higher and higher | childish | continued to climb |
| get bigger | imprecise | increased / expanded |

Task 1 prefers clear academic description, not drama.

## 11. Mini model: same data, Band 6 vs Band 7 lexis

**Data story:** Coffee shop numbers in City A: 120 (2010) → 150 (2015) → 240 (2020). City B: 200 → 190 → 160.

**Band 6 style:**  
> The number of coffee shops in City A increased from 120 to 150 and then increased again to 240. In City B, the number decreased from 200 to 190 and decreased to 160.

**Band 7 style:**  
> City A saw steady growth in coffee shop numbers from 120 to 150, followed by a sharp rise to 240 by 2020. By contrast, City B experienced a gradual decline over the same period, falling from 200 to 160.

Same data; better variety and comparison.

## 12. A practice routine that sticks

**Day 1–2:** Build a personal list of 25 trend phrases in three columns: up / down / stable.  
**Day 3:** Describe one line graph using *no* repeats of *increase* or *decrease* as verbs (nouns allowed once each).  
**Day 4:** Add comparisons and an overview.  
**Day 5:** Submit to an Academic Task 1 check via the [essay checker](/ielts-essay-checker) (select Task 1) and note Lexical Resource comments.  
**Day 6:** Rewrite only the overview and one body paragraph.  
**Day 7:** Timed Task 1 (20 minutes) and compare repetition count.

## Common Task 1 vocabulary mistakes

1. Describing every year instead of selecting key trends  
2. Using *percent* and *percentage points* interchangeably  
3. Inventing reasons (*because of the economy*) — Task 1 is description, not opinion  
4. Copying the question rubric word-for-word without paraphrase  
5. Mixing past and present tense randomly (match the time period on the chart)
