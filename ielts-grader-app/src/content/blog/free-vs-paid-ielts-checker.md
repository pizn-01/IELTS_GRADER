# Free vs Paid IELTS Essay Checker

Do you need a paid IELTS Writing checker, or is a free evaluation enough? The honest answer depends on how often you practise and how close your test date is, not on marketing pressure.

This comparison from **IELTS AI Tutor by IELTSGRADER** explains what free access is for, when paid practice makes sense, and what to demand from any checker (free or paid).

## Quick comparison

| Need | Free check | Paid practice plans |
|------------------|---------------------|
| See how feedback looks | Yes | Yes |
| Baseline diagnosis | Yes | Yes |
| Weekly timed essays with reviews | Limited | Designed for this |
| Mock → report → sprint loops | Limited | Supported |
| Tracking patterns across many scripts | Limited | Stronger fit |
| Last-minute one-off curiosity | Enough | Optional |

## What a free tier is good for

With **IELTS AI Tutor**, free access is ideal when you want to:

- Experience criterion-level feedback (not only a number)
- Understand fix-card style guidance
- Diagnose your baseline before committing to a routine
- Check whether the tutor approach fits your learning style

A free evaluation can answer: “What is my weakest criterion right now?” That alone can shape two weeks of smart practice.

Try the [essay checker](/ielts-essay-checker) or [Task 2 checker](/ielts-task-2-checker).

## What free cannot replace

If your goal is Band improvement, you need **repetition**:

- Multiple timed essays  
- Rewrites after feedback  
- Task 1 / GT letters as well as Task 2  
- Occasional full mocks  

One report is a snapshot. Progress is a trend line. That is when paid weekly or monthly practice becomes practical, not because free feedback is “fake,” but because learning needs volume.

## When paid makes sense

### 1. You can practise 2+ times per week

If you will write regularly, paying for a plan that matches that volume is usually more rational than hunting random free tools with shallow feedback.

### 2. Your test date is within about 8 weeks

Short timelines reward structured loops: mock, plan, drill, retest. See [mock writing practice](/blog/ielts-mock-writing-practice-guide) and [mock to 14-day sprint](/blog/mock-exam-to-14-day-sprint).

### 3. You are stuck at 6 or 6.5

Plateaus break when feedback is frequent enough to correct the same mistake family repeatedly. Read [stuck at Band 6.5](/blog/stuck-at-band-6-5-plateau).

### 4. You want study plans, not only scores

Tutor products turn reports into focus areas and learning editions. That is different from a one-time grade. See [feedback to study plan](/blog/feedback-to-study-plan) and [personalized learning editions](/blog/personalized-learning-editions-guide).

## What to avoid in any checker (free or paid)

- **Score only**, no criterion reasons  
- **Guaranteed band promises**, no ethical tool can guarantee your official result  
- **Template farms**, memorised essays that ignore the question  
- **No Task 1 / letter support** if you need those tasks  
- **Unclear limits**, you cannot plan practice if you do not know what you get

## Free vs paid decision tree

1. Have you ever seen criterion-level feedback on your writing?  
   - **No** → start free.  
   - **Yes** → go to 2.  
2. Will you write at least two checked tasks per week for the next month?  
   - **No** → stay free + self-drills; reconsider later.  
   - **Yes** → go to 3.  
3. Is your test within two months, or are you plateaued?  
   - **Yes** → paid practice plan is usually worth it.  
   - **No** → free + occasional paid bursts can work.

## Cost-effectiveness tip

Paid access is wasted if you:

- Submit essays but never rewrite  
- Ignore the lowest criterion  
- Change topics constantly without fixing patterns  

Paid access is effective if you:

- Follow a weekly plan from each report  
- Combine mocks with drills  
- Track criterion trends  

In other words, the plan matters more than the receipt.

## AI checker vs human lessons (budget angle)

If your budget allows only one paid channel:

- Need **volume of Writing feedback** → AI tutor plans  
- Need **Speaking + live coaching** → human tutor  
- Ideal → hybrid (see [AI vs human tutor](/blog/ai-tutor-vs-human-tutor))  

Do not pay twice for the same unused service.

## A sensible first week (free)

Day 1: free essay check; note lowest criterion.  
Days 2–5: drills only for that criterion.  
Day 6: rewrite.  
Day 7: new timed essay; if you need another full check and free limits are used, decide whether a plan matches your next month.

## Paid plan ROI checklist (use before renewing)

Ask after 14 days:

- Did my lowest criterion move on at least one retest?  
- Did I complete rewrites, or only submissions?  
- Did I practise the Task 1 my module requires?  
- Do I still have a test date that justifies continued intensity?  

If you submitted without rewriting, renewing will not help. If you followed the loop and trends improved, continued access is doing its job. Educational framing: [practice plans explained](/blog/ielts-writing-practice-plans-explained).

## Free tools vs free tiers of tutor products

A random “free band score” site that returns one number is not the same as a free tier of a criterion-based tutor. When comparing free options, prefer:

- Four criteria  
- Explanations  
- Honest practice disclaimers  

Over:

- Instant overall band with no rationale  
- Pressure to upgrade before you have seen feedback quality

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a free IELTS checker accurate enough?

Useful for rough criterion direction; treat scores as practice estimates, not exam results.

### When is paid worth it?

When you need deeper rewrite guidance, history/trends, or higher practice volume.

### Is ChatGPT a free checker?

It can comment on writing, but it is not a calibrated IELTS scoring system by default.
