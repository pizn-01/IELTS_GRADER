# Communities Playbook — Beyond Reddit

## Facebook IELTS groups

**Search:** "IELTS preparation", "IELTS writing", "IELTS study group"

**Approach:**
1. Join 3–5 active groups
2. Lurk 1 week — note common questions
3. Answer 2–3 questions/week with genuine advice (paste short examples)
4. Share blog posts only when directly answering a question
5. Never post bare links

**Post format that works:**
> Someone asked about Band 6 → 7 in Task 2. The biggest leap is usually fully developed body paragraphs. Here's a quick before/after on topic sentences: [paste example]. I wrote a longer guide here if helpful: [blog link]

## Quora

**Target questions:**
- "How can I improve IELTS writing from 6 to 7?"
- "Is AI good for IELTS essay checking?"
- "How is IELTS writing scored?"

**Answer structure:**
1. Direct answer in first 2 sentences
2. Numbered actionable tips
3. Brief personal/tool mention only at end with disclosure

## Discord / Telegram

Search IELTS prep servers. Offer free paragraph feedback in designated help channels. Build reputation before mentioning product.

## YouTube comments

On popular IELTS tutor videos, leave helpful clarifications (not "use my tool"). Link only if creator/community allows resource sharing.

## University / agency partners

Language schools and study-abroad agents often want free tools for students. Offer:
- Free credits for their cohort
- Co-branded landing page (later — G6)

## Weekly checklist

- [ ] 3 helpful comments (Reddit/FB/Quora)
- [ ] 0 promotional posts with no teaching content
- [ ] Log questions you couldn't answer → new blog topics

## Tracker

**G2** — mark progress when you've been active in 2+ communities for 2 weeks.
